# Experiment C: Monumental Trunk Development

## Purpose

We have established a convincing recursive Tree with explicit parent-child topology, monumental graphite geometry and independently routed information packets.

The next development focus is the **trunk**.

The existing trunk is structurally convincing but remains comparatively simple as a transport mechanism. Information converges into it, yet the internal organisation and transformations associated with that convergence are not sufficiently apparent.

We want to investigate three connected properties:

1. **Structural complexity** — subsidiary conduits, nested internal structures, junctions and organisation at multiple scales.
2. **Spatial visibility** — genuine depth and selective visibility of internal structures without sacrificing the monumental graphite exterior.
3. **Transport confluence** — independent information streams entering, sharing and potentially transforming within the trunk.

The central question is:

> Can the trunk become a visibly intricate transport and transformation structure, rather than simply the final common conduit of the Tree?

## Required investigation

Read the current Tree concept, Work Plan, implementation and `routed-light-investigation.md`.

Inspect the existing monumental trunk construction and distributed packet transport before proposing changes.

In particular:

- Establish what internal geometry already exists and how the accepted trunk is constructed.
- Investigate the current `GreaterDepth` containment technique and its camera-dependent limitations.
- Consider approaches for revealing internal structure through a predominantly opaque graphite exterior.
- Investigate whether internal transport conduits can correspond to genuine authored hierarchy rather than decorative branching.
- Examine how multiple independently routed packets could converge without prematurely losing their identities.
- Consider the artistic consequences of substantially increased packet frequency.
- Identify likely geometry, rendering and performance constraints.

## Design principles

Preserve the accepted Tree silhouette, topology, growth behaviour and established materials unless a proposed change has a clear justification.

The trunk must remain a monumental engineered organism, not become a transparent pipe, illuminated circuit board or conventional data visualisation.

Internal complexity must have structural or transport meaning. Do not introduce arbitrary filaments, decorative nodes or glowing patterns merely to increase visual density.

Distinguish three separate mechanisms:

- **Connectivity:** which nodes and conduits are actually connected.
- **Transport:** how independent events travel through those connections.
- **Transformation:** what happens when events meet at a genuine confluence.

Do not assume that visual overlap constitutes semantic aggregation.

The trunk should become more interesting under close inspection while retaining a coherent distant silhouette.

## Scope

This session is **reconnaissance and design review only**.

Do not modify implementation code.

Do not introduce the Casting Pit, Cosmos, lava, glyphs, Category Tree business semantics or new activity mechanisms during this review.

Do not commit to a new rendering architecture before comparing feasible approaches.
