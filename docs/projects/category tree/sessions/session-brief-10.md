# Gallery — Casting Pit v0.4b: Cosmic Circuitry

## Purpose

Refine the existing v0.4a Cosmic Circuitry experiment.

v0.4a established that a persistent golden computational substrate with fast route-bound communication can coexist convincingly with the Casting Pit surface.

However, its underlying generative grammar remains too strongly based on concentric rings and radial connections. The result can read as an astrolabe, distorted spider's web, or decorative network centred upon the gravity well.

v0.4b should address that underlying structural problem.

This is not primarily a rendering-polish exercise.

The central question is:

> **Can ordered mathematical structure become locally intricate and metropolitan at several scales without losing its sense of belonging to a vastly greater Cosmos?**

## Conceptual context — Universe, Cosmos and Worlds

There is a broader artistic idea behind separating the molten and circuitry systems.

For this project we are using an old distinction as a useful metaphor.

### Universe

Universe represents the material world: matter, energy, movement, contingency and physical interaction.

In the Casting Pit this is represented by the molten material.

Its behaviour is governed by physical circumstances:

- inertia;
- gravity;
- collision and interference;
- accumulation;
- obstruction;
- release;
- acceleration as material falls into the well.

Its routes emerge from events rather than being prescribed.

### Cosmos

Cosmos represents underlying mathematical order: relationship, structure, proportion, connectivity and the possibility of organisation.

The golden circuitry belongs to this layer.

Its behaviour must therefore be fundamentally different from the molten material.

The circuitry is not attracted towards the gravity well.

Its topology should not be generated by the well.

Information travels because a connection exists between places, not because a physical force pulls it between them.

Information packets should therefore move rapidly and at essentially constant propagation speed along the network. A route may cross level territory, descend into the well, pass through it or avoid it entirely without changing speed because of gravity.

A useful distinction is:

> **Matter falls. Information is routed.**

Nevertheless, Universe and Cosmos inhabit the same reality. They are not intended ultimately to appear as unrelated transparent layers.

The working proposition is:

> **The Cosmos orders the Universe and thereby facilitates the creation of Worlds.**

"Worlds" here means more than physical planets. It refers to recursively produced structures of existence: organisms, technologies, artefacts, societies, lives, knowledge and stories — things which themselves participate in further production.

This is not a request to illustrate that proposition literally or turn the scene into a philosophical diagram.

It explains why the circuitry must possess genuine order rather than merely decorative complexity.

## Topology versus manifestation

The logical topology of the Cosmos must be generated independently of the Casting Pit.

However, this does not mean the visible circuitry must ignore the physical surface.

The distinction is:

- **Topology** determines what is connected to what.
- **Manifestation** determines how that topology appears within the shared physical scene.

The Casting Pit must not create, organise or reroute the logical network.

But the visible manifestation of that network may encode or respond to the deformation of the surface.

For example, circuitry may descend with the membrane, bend spatially with it, vary in apparent spacing, or otherwise reveal the geometry it inhabits.

This should be treated as a relationship between two systems sharing the same reality, not as gravity acting upon information.

A useful rule is:

> **The pit may distort the manifestation of Cosmos, but it must not determine the topology of Cosmos.**

## What v0.4a established

Retain the successful principles of v0.4a:

- circuitry has its own logical 2D coordinate system;
- spatial projection onto the Casting Pit is separate from network topology;
- the network is an explicit graph with real adjacency;
- communication follows graph routes;
- communication distance is measured along those routes;
- the graphite/gold/ember visual language is viable;
- dark groove shoulders and hotter conductive cores are useful;
- persistent low-level network activity makes the substrate feel alive;
- travelling white-gold electrical discharges can represent information;
- `Surface binding` is a useful experimental control;
- circuitry extends conceptually beyond the visible territory;
- diagnostics remain diagnostics rather than artwork.

Do not discard these successes merely to obtain a different pattern.

---

## Principal weakness to remove

v0.4a is fundamentally ring-first.

Concentric bands are generated around the centre and radial, chord and trunk structures are subsequently added to them.

Consequently the gravity well remains the implicit organising centre of the Cosmos even when later rendering and hierarchy treatments attempt to disguise it.

This is conceptually wrong.

The Casting Pit is a local event occurring within the Cosmos.

> **The visible scene samples the Cosmos; the pit does not compose it.**

v0.4b should therefore replace the ring-centred generative grammar.

Do not solve the problem merely by:

- adding more lines;
- increasing random variation;
- distorting the existing rings further;
- adding more glow;
- increasing communication density;
- hiding the radial structure beneath visual noise.

The objective is not greater complexity at one scale.

It is **order at many scales**.

## Desired generative character

Think more in terms of extremely intricate circuitry than a web.

The network should contain several scales of organisation.

For example:

- a small number of major conduits or structural routes;
- significant junctions and branching structures;
- secondary distribution systems;
- locally dense districts;
- subsidiary networks within those districts;
- very fine local connections;
- sparse or almost empty territories;
- incomplete structures continuing beyond the visible scene.

These examples describe structural roles, not a prescribed visual layout.

There should be multiple local centres of organisation.

The network must not globally converge upon `(0,0)` or otherwise reveal the gravity well as its organising principle.

Some major structures may enter the visible territory from one edge and leave another. A dense district might occur partly outside the frame. Another region may contain only a few great conduits.

The visible scene should feel like a small observation window onto something enormously larger.

A useful visual analogy is an aerial view of an impossibly intricate computational city, but it must remain abstract enough to belong to the existing Gallery language.

Do not literally draw buildings, roads, circuit boards or a city.

---

## Recursive / hierarchical generation

Investigate a generative grammar in which large-scale structures create conditions for progressively smaller structures.

The exact algorithm is for this experiment to discover, but the important property is hierarchy.

For example:

> major structure → secondary structure → local district → subsidiary network → fine connectivity

This need not be a literal tree.

Avoid replacing the concentric web with another obvious single mathematical primitive.

The result should contain recognisable organisation at several spatial scales.

Variation should primarily arise from the generative hierarchy rather than arbitrary positional noise.

---

## Relationship with the gravity well

Generate the logical Cosmos independently of the Casting Pit.

Only after the graph exists should its logical positions be manifested spatially against the surface.

Retain `Surface binding` or an equivalent experimental mechanism so that we can continue investigating the relationship between mathematical order and physical deformation.

The well may spatially deform the visible manifestation of circuitry.

It must not determine its topology.

Some network routes should naturally cross the well region because the independent network happens to pass through that territory.

Others should bypass it.

Do not deliberately route every important conduit towards the centre.

---

## Communication behaviour

Retain explicit graph-based communication.

Information should:

- follow connectivity;
- move rapidly;
- maintain essentially constant propagation speed along route distance;
- ignore gravity as a causal force;
- cross junctions coherently;
- be capable of travelling through circuitry manifested on the slopes or inside the well without accelerating towards its centre.

The visual distinction from future molten material is important:

- molten matter will move comparatively slowly and accelerate as physical forces act upon it;
- information should propagate rapidly and consistently because it is being routed.

Do not add fluid-like acceleration, attraction or momentum to information packets.

The communication system should reveal the network rather than dominate it.

## Scope

### In scope

- replacing the v0.4a ring-first graph generator;
- hierarchical/multi-scale network generation;
- multiple local organisational centres;
- major, intermediate and fine network structures;
- spatially varied density;
- continuation beyond the visible territory;
- reuse/refinement of v0.4a circuitry rendering;
- reuse of graph-routed communication;
- constant-speed information propagation;
- existing membrane, gravity well, camera and diagnostics;
- controls needed to judge the new structural model.

### Out of scope

Do not add:

- lava;
- peripheral streams;
- reservoirs;
- sluice gates;
- the central throat;
- the Tree;
- Category Tree semantics;
- literal buildings or city geometry;
- a full circuit simulation;
- a new physical particle simulation;
- post-processing intended to conceal weak geometry/topology.

The eventual combination of Universe and Cosmos is a later experiment.

v0.4b must first demonstrate that Cosmos works independently.

## Visual acceptance criteria

The experiment succeeds if:

1. The first impression is no longer concentric rings, spider web or astrolabe.
2. The gravity well does not appear to be the network's organising centre.
3. The circuitry visibly contains several scales of organisation.
4. Some regions feel densely structured while others remain comparatively sparse.
5. The visible network plausibly continues beyond the scene.
6. The result reads as ordered computational circuitry rather than random line noise.
7. Major and minor structures belong recognisably to the same system.
8. Communication clearly travels through actual network connectivity.
9. Information maintains fast, essentially constant route speed irrespective of the gravity well.
10. The graphite/gold living-substrate character established by v0.4a is retained or improved.
11. The scene remains visually compelling with communication reduced or temporarily disabled; moving packets must not be required to disguise weak topology.
12. The result suggests a small visible fragment of a much greater ordered system.

The most important judgement is:

> **Does this look like order at many scales, rather than complexity at one scale?**
