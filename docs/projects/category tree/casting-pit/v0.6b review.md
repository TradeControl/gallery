# Casting Pit v0.6b review — canonical well frame and historical throat

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Status:** design and diagnostic review; no implementation authorised by this document

## Assessment

The v0.6a experiment established two useful facts.

First, replacing the former receiver disc with a genuine opening and throat removed the conspicuous "teacup rim" and made the well read as a cavity. Second, the erosion controls are not wholly inert: after the earlier control-update fault was corrected, flow erosion in particular produces a discernible change.

The remaining problem is more fundamental than insufficient erosion strength. The unusual form visible inside the well is consistent with several parts of the construction using related but different centres. The Gaussian depression and the molten approach still converge on the original world origin, while the opening is evaluated around a seeded offset and the throat moves farther away from that frame as it descends. The result is not simply an asymmetric eroded throat. It is a compound displacement through depth, seen inside a well whose larger physical field still implies a different axis.

This changes the priority for v0.6b. Strengthening the erosion or widening its controls before resolving the coordinate chain would exaggerate an accidental anomaly. The next experiment should establish one explicit well frame, remove unintended centre drift and only then reassess the artistic character of the throat.

The erosion itself should remain historical rather than animated. The controls were valuable diagnostic instruments, but they should be understood as selecting alternate accumulated histories, not as simulating erosion occurring while the scene runs.

## Reconnaissance findings

The current coordinate chain contains the following relationships.

| Element | Centre or frame currently used | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Former receiver mesh | World `(0, 0)` | The old visual receiver was centred on the original well axis. It is not rendered when the throat opening is active. |
| Former radial parcel capture | World `(0, 0)` | This is now only a fallback when no opening predicate exists. It is not the active capture rule in v0.6a. |
| Gaussian minimum | World `(0, 0)` | The base depression is calculated from `x² + z²`, so its mathematical minimum remains at the original origin. |
| Molten convergence | World `(0, 0)` in the inherited physical system | The established field and approach behaviour still visually reinforce the original Gaussian axis. |
| Opening predicate | Seeded local frame offset from `(0, 0)` | `localPolar()` subtracts `centerOffset()` before evaluating the eroded mouth. At the default history this offset is about `(-0.088, -0.025)`. |
| Opening area centroid | Derived from the asymmetric contour | At the default history it is approximately `(-0.144, 0.018)`. This need not equal the frame centre and is not itself evidence of a defect. |
| Molten mask | Samples the opening predicate | It is aligned with the opening predicate. Its small difference from the analytic centroid is consistent with the mask resolution. |
| Parcel capture | Samples the opening predicate along each parcel step | It is aligned with the opening predicate and does not use the obsolete receiver rule while the throat is active. |
| Throat mouth | Opening frame plus the eroded contour and a seam overlap | The upper ring broadly follows the same mouth, with deliberate overlap to hide a seam. |
| Throat centreline | Drifts farther with depth | A separate `bottomShift` increases with throat depth, compounding the initial opening offset. |
| Flow-driven terrain cuts | Angles and radii calculated around world `(0, 0)` | These do not use the offset opening frame, creating a second disagreement inside the erosion model. |

At the default settings, the mean centre of the top throat ring is approximately `(-0.115, -0.002)` and the bottom ring approximately `(-0.192, -0.044)`. At maximum background erosion, the opening-frame displacement is approximately `0.19` world units and the lower throat displacement can reach approximately `0.38` world units. That is substantial relative to the constricted lower aperture.

The investigation found no evidence of an axis swap, sign reversal or object-transform mismatch. The surface, mask and parcels all operate in compatible material/world `x,z` coordinates. The principal issue is therefore not a broken texture lookup or capture test. It is the use of more than one conceptual centre.

## Explanation of the throat anomaly

An eroded mouth may legitimately be non-circular, unbalanced and even have an area centroid that lies away from the nominal centre. Those qualities are part of the intended accumulated history. They should not be confused with translating the coordinate system used to construct the mouth.

The current implementation combines three different effects:

1. **Contour asymmetry** changes the radius of the opening by angle. This is desirable.
2. **Opening-frame translation** moves the polar origin used to evaluate that contour away from the Gaussian minimum. This is not presently justified by the physical model.
3. **Depth-dependent throat translation** moves the lower aperture farther again. This creates an unintended sloping or kinked throat whose axis is not shared by the visible gravity well.

The first effect communicates erosion. The second and third together create the anomalous dark core. From an oblique view, the viewer sees the Gaussian bowl and molten flow converging on one location while the aperture and its lower termination imply another. The seam overlap and throat shading make the disagreement more conspicuous, but they are not its primary cause.

The flow-driven cuts add a related inconsistency. They are oriented around the global origin, whereas the opening boundary is evaluated around the displaced local frame. Consequently a historical approach direction can cut the terrain in one coordinate system and recess the mouth in another.

This also explains why the sliders initially appeared ineffective. Some geometric changes were subtle in the chosen view, while the stronger visual signal was the persistent internal misalignment. Increasing the parameters changed the eroded form but did not resolve the contradiction at its centre; at extremes it could increase it.

## Proposed v0.6b model

v0.6b should introduce one explicit **canonical well frame** shared by every part of the cavity construction. For the bounded experiment, its centre should remain the existing Gaussian minimum at world `(0, 0)`.

That decision does not require a circular or symmetrical mouth. It separates two concerns:

- the **frame** states where the well is and supplies the origin from which radius, angle and depth are measured;
- the **historical profile** states how erosion has distorted the material around that frame.

Background erosion should continue to produce low-frequency radial recession, shelves and unequal sectors. Flow-driven erosion should continue to produce a small number of gate-derived dominant cuts. Both should modify the profile around the canonical frame rather than move the frame itself.

The first v0.6b throat should have a straight, shared centreline. A deliberately curved or undercut throat may later prove artistically valuable, but it should be introduced as an explicit physical feature after the aligned version has been assessed. It should not emerge indirectly from the background-erosion amplitude.

## Recommended remediation

### Stage 1 — make the coordinate contract explicit

Before changing the appearance, define a small well-frame value owned by the membrane/world mechanism, containing at least the canonical `x,z` centre. Use it consistently for:

- the Gaussian base depression;
- opening polar coordinates;
- background erosion;
- flow-driven channels and mouth recession;
- throat-ring construction;
- diagnostic measurements;
- any future receiver or below-mouth behaviour.

The molten mask and parcel capture should continue to call the opening predicate rather than reimplementing the shape. Their current shared-predicate relationship is sound and should be preserved.

### Stage 2 — remove the accidental displacement

Apply the smallest geometry correction capable of proving the diagnosis:

1. Remove the erosion-dependent translation from the polar frame used by the opening.
2. Preserve asymmetry through angular radius changes, shelves and recessed sectors.
3. Remove the erosion-dependent `bottomShift` from the initial throat implementation so the top and bottom rings share the canonical axis.
4. Evaluate flow-channel radius and angle in the same canonical frame as the opening.
5. Retain the radial seam overlap, but measure it from that same frame.
6. Leave the Gaussian equation, molten dynamics, reservoir, gates, parcel integration and capture sequence otherwise unchanged.

This is deliberately a correction rather than a redesign. No erosion amplitude should be strengthened during this stage.

### Stage 3 — prove alignment numerically and visually

Add temporary diagnostics that distinguish between a construction frame and the centroid of an asymmetric shape. Useful measurements are:

- canonical well centre;
- Gaussian minimum;
- opening polar-frame centre;
- opening area centroid;
- molten-mask centroid;
- top-ring mean centre;
- bottom-ring mean centre;
- the bearings of historical flow approaches.

The opening and mask centroids may differ from the canonical centre because an asymmetric area has an asymmetric centroid. That is acceptable. The opening frame and throat-ring centreline should not drift.

For visual inspection, compare the same seeded history in aerial, elevated and monumental views, plus a free orbit around the mouth. A temporary centreline or small diagnostic markers may be useful while proving the geometry, provided they are disabled in the normal artistic view.

### Stage 4 — reassess the historical form

Only after the anomaly is absent should the throat be judged artistically. At that point the experiment can determine whether it needs:

- stronger background recession;
- more legible flow-derived cuts;
- a deliberately undercut or curved throat;
- revised lighting or normals at the mouth seam.

These are separate questions. They should not be used to compensate for a coordinate disagreement.

## Treatment of the controls

The current controls should not be removed before v0.6b equivalence testing. Their extremes are useful for exposing discontinuities and proving that the coordinate frame remains stable.

Their meaning should, however, be clarified:

- **Background erosion** selects the intensity of a pre-existing, accumulated terrain history.
- **Flow imprint** selects how strongly repeated historical gate approaches are recorded in that terrain.
- **History seed** selects another deterministic history.
- **Mouth scale**, **throat constriction** and **throat depth** remain geometric study controls rather than erosion over time.

Changing one of these values rebuilds a static historical state. It does not represent erosion occurring during a molten release.

Once the corrected throat has been assessed, it may be artistically preferable to replace continuous erosion sliders with a few curated deterministic histories, while retaining detailed controls in a diagnostic mode. That decision should be deferred. Sliders are not the cause of the current anomaly, and removing them now would reduce our ability to test the correction.

## Approaches considered

### A. Canonical straight throat — recommended

Keep the Gaussian minimum, opening frame and throat centreline on one explicit axis. Express all historical irregularity in the surrounding profile.

This is the smallest correction, the clearest diagnostic and the best baseline for later artistic decisions.

### B. Deliberately curved historical throat — defer

A long-lived flow could plausibly undercut a throat or bend its passage. This may eventually produce a richer result, but it would require a designed centreline, a credible relationship to approach directions and careful treatment of capture below the mouth.

The current `bottomShift` does not establish that model; it merely scales a seeded offset with background erosion. Retaining it would prevent us from knowing whether the anomaly has been solved.

### C. Move the Gaussian and molten convergence to the displaced opening — not recommended for v0.6b

The systems could theoretically be re-centred under the current opening. That would alter the established gravity field and physical composition for the sake of preserving an incidental offset. It is broader in scope and risks changing the signed-off physical behaviour.

### D. Keep the current coordinate chain and improve shading — rejected

Lighting, normals and seam treatment could disguise parts of the core, but they would not resolve the competing axes. This would make later layering less dependable.

## Transport invariants

The correction must preserve the established causal sequence:

```text
incoming streams
    → shared reservoir
    → selected gates
    → released molten material
    → gravity well
    → eroded throat
```

In particular:

- the throat must not create a material source;
- the opening must not allow reservoir material to bypass the selected gates;
- the existing parcel motion and emergent molten reconstruction must remain unchanged;
- material is removed only after a released parcel crosses the opening predicate;
- the former receiver disc and radial fallback must remain inactive while the opening is present;
- no below-mouth fluid or wall-flow simulation is introduced.

## Acceptance criteria

v0.6b should be accepted only if all of the following are true.

### Coordinate and geometry

- A single canonical well centre is explicit in the implementation.
- The Gaussian minimum, opening polar frame and throat centreline use that centre.
- The top- and bottom-ring mean centres agree with the canonical axis within `0.01` world units under minimum, default and maximum erosion settings.
- Flow-driven cuts use the same centre when deriving their radius and angle.
- The mouth remains visibly irregular and non-circular; alignment must not erase historical character.
- The seam remains closed without introducing a raised rim.

### Mask and capture

- The molten mask is generated from the same opening predicate used by parcel capture.
- Its measured centroid agrees with a sampled analytic opening centroid to within one mask texel, while recognising that both may legitimately differ from the canonical centre.
- Synthetic parcel segments crossing the opening are captured, including segments that pass across it between frames.
- Parcels outside the opening are not captured merely because they approach the old receiver radius.

### Visual review

- The dark internal core no longer appears detached, kinked or eccentrically pinned to the mouth in aerial view.
- The same alignment remains credible in elevated, monumental and freely orbited views.
- Background and flow-driven erosion remain independently discernible at diagnostic extremes.
- No visual strengthening is required merely to prove that a control responds.

### Preservation

- The signed-off reservoir, gate selection, release timing, molten reconstruction and camera behaviour remain materially unchanged.
- No new leakage or uncontrolled wastage into the well is introduced.
- No changes are made to Cosmos manifestation, information-layer behaviour, the Tree or the Category Tree.

## Scope boundary

This review proposes a bounded throat correction. It does not authorise:

- dynamic terrain erosion;
- stronger or more numerous erosion features before alignment is proved;
- a new molten transport mechanism;
- wall-bound or falling-fluid simulation below the mouth;
- post-processing intended to conceal the anomaly;
- changes to the information layer or its interaction with the physical layer;
- Tree or Category Tree work.

Mesh resolution, polygonal mouth edges and detailed throat normals remain legitimate later refinements, but they should not be conflated with the centre anomaly.

## Recommendation

Proceed with **Approach A: canonical straight throat** as a diagnostic v0.6b implementation.

The working hypothesis is precise and testable: the anomalous well core is caused principally by an opening frame displaced from the Gaussian and molten convergence, compounded by a throat centreline that drifts farther with depth. The opening mask and parcel capture already agree with one another and should not be redesigned.

Correct the coordinate frame first, preserve the asymmetric historical profile, and verify the result at parameter extremes. If the core then reads coherently, the aligned implementation will provide a trustworthy base from which to decide whether a deliberately curved historical throat or stronger erosion is artistically warranted.
