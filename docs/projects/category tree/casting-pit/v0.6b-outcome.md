# Casting Pit v0.6b — canonical well frame outcome

**Date:** 17 September 2026
**Status:** coordinate correction implemented and verified; visual objective not achieved

## Outcome

v0.6b successfully removed the unintended disagreement between the mathematical centres used by the Gaussian depression, eroded opening, historical flow cuts and throat axis. It did not, however, remove the conspicuous dark anomaly inside the well.

The experiment must therefore be regarded as a useful but incomplete correction rather than a successful throat treatment. The coordinate investigation identified and repaired a real defect, but that defect was not the dominant cause of the visible black central form. The subsequent aerial review showed that the stronger problem is the exposed lower termination of the throat mesh.

The user was not seeing an obsolete page, cached implementation or inactive control. The v0.6b implementation was running as intended and its remaining anomaly was produced by the geometry itself.

## What was implemented

The work followed the recommended canonical-frame approach in `v0.6b review.md`.

### One explicit well frame

`createErodedThroatMembrane()` now owns a canonical well frame whose default centre is world `(0, 0)`. The following mechanisms all derive their position from that frame:

- the Gaussian base depression;
- the polar coordinates used by the opening predicate;
- background erosion;
- flow-channel radius and angle;
- gate-derived historical approach guides;
- the throat rings and authored throat axis;
- alignment diagnostics.

The erosion-dependent `centerOffset()` was removed. Background erosion continues to alter the radial profile, but it no longer translates the coordinate system in which that profile is evaluated.

### Straight authored throat axis

The depth-dependent `bottomShift` was removed. Every throat ring is now constructed around the same canonical axis instead of moving farther from the Gaussian centre as it descends.

This did not make the mouth circular. Its radius still varies by angle through the deterministic background profile, flow recession and small depth-dependent twist. Its area centroid and the arithmetic means of its vertices may therefore remain away from `(0, 0)`. That is legitimate contour asymmetry rather than a second coordinate frame.

### Flow erosion in the same frame

Flow-channel radius and angle are now evaluated after conversion to the canonical local frame. The historical cuts and the opening no longer use different origins.

At the present default centre of `(0, 0)`, some numerical expressions give the same values as their former global calculations. Making this relationship explicit nevertheless prevents the systems from diverging again and keeps the geometry internally coherent.

### Diagnostic facilities

The membrane now exposes alignment measurements for:

- canonical centre;
- Gaussian minimum;
- opening-frame centre;
- upper and lower throat axes;
- opening-area centroid;
- upper and lower ring means.

An optional cyan throat centreline and crosshair were added to the existing surface diagnostic mode. They are hidden during the normal artistic presentation and introduce no new visual layer into the finished view.

The experiment page was relabelled **Casting Pit v0.6b — Canonical Well Throat** so it can be distinguished from v0.6a during review.

## What was deliberately preserved

No changes were made to the established transport sequence:

```text
incoming streams
    → shared reservoir
    → selected gates
    → released molten material
    → gravity well
    → eroded throat
```

The following behaviour remains as before:

- gate selection, storage and release timing;
- parcel emission, interaction and surface forces;
- molten deposition and reconstruction;
- the shared opening predicate used by both masking and parcel capture;
- swept-segment capture that prevents parcels crossing the opening between samples;
- camera controls and physical-layer lighting;
- the absence of below-mouth fluid simulation.

The former receiver mesh and radial receiver capture remain inactive whenever the throat opening is present. The dark object seen in the reviewed image is not the old receiver.

## Verification performed

The coordinate contract was tested at all nine combinations formed by minimum, default and maximum background erosion and flow imprint values.

In every combination:

- the canonical centre was `(0, 0)`;
- the Gaussian minimum was `(0, 0)`;
- the opening polar frame was centred at `(0, 0)`;
- the authored upper and lower throat axes were centred at `(0, 0)`;
- the origin remained inside the opening;
- a segment crossing the opening was captured by the swept predicate.

The live experiment was also inspected in the browser at default values and with both erosion controls at their maximum. The controls rebuilt the geometry, the optional centreline remained aligned through the throat and no browser warnings or errors were produced.

These checks prove that the intended coordinate correction was applied. They do not prove that the resulting throat is visually successful.

## Visual review

The subsequent aerial screenshot showed substantially the same black, irregular form at the core of the well as v0.6a. The coordinate correction may have made its placement more coherent, but it did not remove the feature or materially improve the first visual impression.

This failed the important v0.6b acceptance criterion that the dark internal core should no longer appear as a detached or anomalous object. The implementation should not be described as a successful remedy on the strength of its numerical alignment alone.

## Why the implementation did not work visually

The throat is currently an open-sided tube constructed from nine rings: an upper ring, a lower ring and seven intermediate rings. Triangles connect adjacent rings to form the wall. No geometry continues below the final ring, and there is no terminal surface.

At the default settings, the exposed lower boundary has:

- a radius varying approximately from `0.44` to `0.65` world units;
- a height varying approximately from `-8.18` to `-7.65`, a difference of about `0.54` world units;
- 128 open boundary edges;
- no lower cap or continuation.

The height variation occurs because every point down the throat derives its vertical starting position from the eroded surface height at its corresponding upper-rim direction. Although the same throat depth is subtracted, sectors that begin at different rim heights end at different heights as well. The final ring is therefore not a distant, level aperture. It is an uneven and visibly shaped boundary.

From an aerial or steep elevated camera, the viewer can look through that boundary and see the near-black scene background. The dark negative space inherits the asymmetric, constricted outline of the final ring. Lighting on the adjacent double-sided throat wall strengthens its faceted edge, causing the opening to resemble a small black polygonal object or plug sitting at the convergence point.

This visual effect is much stronger than the former coordinate displacement. At the default history, the removed opening-frame offset was only about `0.09` world units and the former lower-axis displacement about `0.19` world units. Correcting those values was worthwhile, but it could not eliminate an exposed aperture roughly half a world unit in radius.

The original diagnosis therefore confused two separate problems:

1. **Coordinate incoherence**, which was real and has now been corrected.
2. **Visible finite termination**, which produces the dominant anomaly and remains unchanged.

The completion report following implementation was too confident because it treated successful alignment as evidence that the visible core had been remedied. The user's aerial review correctly demonstrated that it had not.

## Approaches to a remedy

### A. Continue the throat beyond the visible cavity — recommended

The next bounded experiment should extend the static throat geometry far enough that its terminal boundary cannot be seen from any supported camera position.

This should be a geometric continuation, not a new molten simulation. Its purpose is to make the throat read as passage into depth rather than a short tube ending above the scene background.

The continuation should:

- retain the canonical axis established by v0.6b;
- begin continuously from the existing throat wall;
- become darker with depth through ordinary material and lighting response;
- avoid an observable lower silhouette;
- remain open rather than terminating in a visible disc;
- preserve the current opening predicate and parcel capture plane.

The minimum required length should be derived from the steepest permitted camera view and the lowest practical throat constriction. Merely multiplying the current depth by an arbitrary constant could move the problem without proving that it is outside the view volume.

### B. Make vertical descent converge on a coherent depth profile — recommended with A

The current lower-edge height variation is inherited unchanged from the upper surface. A continued throat should progressively transition from the eroded rim heights towards a coherent axial depth profile.

This does not require making the mouth symmetrical. The upper throat must still meet the irregular terrain. The vertical interpolation can gradually absorb those local height differences as depth increases, preventing them from being reproduced as a jagged terminal boundary.

A useful construction would distinguish:

- the irregular upper attachment height required for a closed seam;
- a smooth descent distance shared by the throat as a whole;
- optional restrained longitudinal variation that does not form a visible terminal rosette.

### C. Add a cap at the present lower ring — not recommended

A dark cap would conceal the scene background but would probably turn the anomaly into a more definite black disc or polygon. A lit cap could resemble a floor, receiver or plug. Either reading would contradict the intended impression of an opening continuing into depth.

A cap may be useful only as a temporary diagnostic: if it exactly replaces the observed shape, it would further confirm that the lower aperture is responsible. It should not be adopted as the artistic solution.

### D. Hide the anomaly through lighting or post-processing — rejected

Darkening the wall, adding fog, bloom or a painted gradient without correcting the finite visible boundary would conceal rather than solve the geometry. Such treatment could also interfere with the later synthesis of Universe and Cosmos.

### E. Introduce falling or wall-bound molten material — out of scope

Molten material descending below the mouth might eventually occupy some of this visual space, but it would be a new transport problem and could merely cover the defect. The throat geometry must first be credible when inspected without that additional layer.

## Proposed next experiment

A v0.6c experiment should remain confined to the physical throat and proceed in two diagnostic passes.

### Pass 1 — prove the exposed termination diagnosis

1. Give the current lower boundary a temporary unmistakable diagnostic colour or wireframe.
2. Confirm in aerial, elevated, monumental and free-orbit views that it exactly surrounds the black anomaly.
3. Temporarily extend the throat substantially while preserving all other parameters.
4. Confirm that the black rosette disappears or retreats from every supported view.
5. Remove the temporary diagnostic treatment.

This pass should not alter erosion amplitude, molten behaviour or lighting.

### Pass 2 — construct the minimal credible continuation

1. Retain the irregular upper seam and canonical axis.
2. Increase vertical subdivisions only as required for a smooth continuation.
3. Gradually reconcile directional rim-height differences with a shared depth profile.
4. Choose a continuation length based on camera visibility tests.
5. Ensure the lower boundary remains outside every supported view at control extremes.
6. Re-test the molten mask and parcel capture without moving their established mouth-level predicate.

Only after those passes succeed should throat constriction, internal curvature, shading or stronger historical erosion be reconsidered artistically.

## Acceptance criteria for the remedy

The next correction should be accepted only if:

- no terminal ring, black rosette, plug or detached polygon is visible through the throat in aerial, elevated, monumental or free-orbit inspection;
- the upper throat remains seamlessly attached to the irregular eroded mouth;
- the canonical frame remains shared by the Gaussian, opening, flow cuts and throat axis;
- the mouth remains historically asymmetric rather than reverting to a circular bore;
- minimum and maximum erosion settings do not reveal a lower termination;
- selected gates remain the only route from the reservoir to released molten material;
- the opening predicate, molten mask and swept parcel capture remain unchanged in meaning;
- no wall-flow or falling-fluid simulation is introduced;
- the correction remains valid with the diagnostic overlay disabled.

## Recommendation

Retain the v0.6b canonical-frame correction. It removes a genuine structural inconsistency and provides the correct basis for further throat work.

Do not attempt to rescue the present image by strengthening erosion, adding a cap or changing the molten layer. The next step should be a narrowly instrumented v0.6c test of the exposed lower boundary, followed by a static throat continuation whose termination lies outside all supported views.

The immediate design question is no longer whether the erosion sliders are responding. It is whether a finite piece of throat geometry can be made to read as an unbounded descent without exposing its construction boundary. That problem should be solved before any additional physical or informational layers are introduced.
