# Casting Pit

## Concept

The Casting Pit is the world from which the Category Tree will emerge: an immense gridded membrane drawn down into a central casting or gravity depression.

At a distance the surface appears ordered, calm and nearly planar. Approaching the centre, that order is placed under visible strain. The membrane bends, stretches and steepens towards a throat, making the invisible force of convergence tangible. The image should recall graph paper laid over spacetime, a flexible industrial floor, a tensioned sheet and the mouth of a foundry without settling into a literal illustration of any one of them.

The grid is essential. It is not a texture effect placed over a crater; it is a material marking attached to the membrane. Its changing squares reveal what the surface is doing. Deformation of surface and deformation of grid must therefore tell the same story. The grid should remain continuous and intelligible even where it becomes compressed, stretched or strongly foreshortened.

## Intended experience

The pit should feel monumental and bodily. A low, human-scale camera can make the horizon and descent physically credible, while an elevated or orbiting inspection view can expose the logic of the deformation. The centre must exert visual authority without turning the entire field into a radial pattern.

The surface should possess industrial material character—dark, mineral, metallic, ceramic or soot-marked—but the first proof is form rather than finish. Light exists to clarify slope, tension and depth. Atmospheric richness should be earned after the membrane reads convincingly.

The depression may begin as a heightfield-like surface, but the artistic destination includes a deliberate central transition: outer membrane, steep convergence, casting mouth or throat, and an implied space below. The throat should ultimately feel like a place where surface-bound material can cease to be surface-bound.

## Relationship to flow

Lava or molten flow is a separate future system. It will form distinct channels or ribbons across the membrane, move consistently downhill, merge as it approaches the centre, and eventually fall or transfer through the throat. It must not be produced by wobbling the grid or by making the whole coordinate field oscillate.

This separation gives each layer a clear role:

- The membrane establishes the world and bears the deformation.
- The attached grid makes that deformation legible.
- The later flow system shows transport and convergence.
- The throat marks the transition from distributed surface movement to concentrated depth.

## Implementation awareness

The concept favours a parametric, subdivided surface whose material coordinates travel with it, because this directly supports the physical reading of a marked flexible sheet. Other techniques remain possible, but intersections, normals and coordinate mappings must be reliable enough that rendering artefacts are not mistaken for deformation.

Live controls should expose perceptual questions: depth and breadth of the depression, the character of its falloff, any inward material contraction, grid density and weight, camera height and distance, and lighting that reveals the form. Controls are instruments for finding the artwork, not an invitation to surface every implementation constant.

## Provenance

The archived Casting Pit shader demonstrated a recognisable gravity-well image, but its geometry, grid warp and animated flow were unrelated transformations. It treated vertical separation from a heightfield as a distance field, introduced unstable intersection behaviour, and asked the grid to perform the work of both deformation and lava. The resulting oval and ring-like distortions are useful evidence of what to avoid.

The archive does not define the new pit's profile, renderer or architecture. Its durable lesson is simpler: geometry, material coordinates and transport must agree where they are related, and remain separate where they are not.
