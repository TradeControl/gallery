# Casting Pit v0.6a review — Well-to-Throat Transition

16 September 2026

## Assessment

The current centre is regular because it contains no throat geometry. It is a smooth Gaussian heightfield terminating perceptually at a separate circular receiver. A convincing eroded transition can be introduced locally without replacing the successful broad well, but a genuine opening cannot remain a heightfield alone.

The smallest useful experiment is a **Universe-only hybrid throat**: preserve the Gaussian membrane outside a compact transition region, cut an irregular opening through its centre, and add a short explicitly modelled throat wall beneath it. Its static form should represent accumulated physical history through both background erosion and a few flow-driven cuts derived from plausible historical gate-release approaches. Continue using the established surface-bound parcel dynamics until material crosses the opening. Do not yet add Cosmos, a falling-fluid phase, the Tree or dynamic terrain erosion.

This would test the important artistic proposition—the progression from monumental well to stressed ground, eroded lip and constricted darkness—without conflating it with later transport below the surface.

## 1. What currently produces the centre

`createGaussianMembrane()` defines one radially symmetric height function:

    y(x, z) = -depth × exp(-(x² + z²) / width²)

The visible membrane and molten overlay each use a 24 × 24 `PlaneGeometry` with 180 subdivisions per side. Their original planar X/Z positions are retained as material coordinates; deformation changes only Y and then recomputes vertex normals. The diagnostic wireframe uses the same mapping at 32 subdivisions.

The Gaussian has its minimum and zero gradient at `(0, 0)`. It therefore produces a smooth bowl with no lip, aperture, vertical wall or undercut.

The apparent termination is supplied separately:

- a flat `CircleGeometry` receiver of radius `0.34`, placed just above the Gaussian minimum;
- a parcel capture test using the same circular radius;
- deletion of parcels after they reach that radius.

The existing central circle is consequently a cap and removal boundary, not a modelled opening. The larger molten rim at approximately radius 5.5–6.5 is the packet-backed sluice reservoir and is unrelated to the unresolved central throat.

## 2. Heightfield assumptions in the physical system

The current physical simulation is fundamentally two-dimensional in material space:

- parcels store X/Z position and velocity only;
- spatial hashing and neighbour interactions use planar X/Z distance;
- gravity is derived from the analytic X/Z gradient of the Gaussian;
- parcel display height is sampled from `membrane.height(x, z)`;
- the reconstruction field is a 224 × 224 texture covering the same fixed X/Z territory;
- deposited density, heat and velocity are advected in that planar texture;
- the molten mesh obtains height by sharing the membrane geometry.

This works because every X/Z location currently has exactly one surface height. A local irregular **top surface** can preserve that assumption. Vertical throat walls, an undercut or the empty aperture cannot: they contain zero or several surface points for one X/Z coordinate.

The useful boundary is therefore clear. Existing parcel dynamics can continue over the single-valued outer membrane and eroded approach. At the aperture, a new predicate should transfer or remove the parcel. Modelling its subsequent fall down a wall would be a separate 3D transport problem and is not required to judge v0.6a geometry.

Other radial assumptions exist but are outside the proposed local region: source emission, the shared reservoir and gates use radii near 5.35–6.55. A throat contained inside roughly radius 1–2 need not disturb those mechanisms.

## 3. Molten reconstruction at the centre

Every moving or stored parcel deposits a trail into the planar reconstruction field. A parcel that enters the receiver deposits through its final integration step and is then removed when its radius is at most `0.34`. The density and heat it left behind continue to advect, spread and decay according to persistence.

The molten shader currently renders that field over a complete membrane mesh. It has no knowledge of the receiver or an opening. This is why the centre can retain an abrupt circular molten termination even after the generating parcel has disappeared.

An irregular opening requires three coordinated changes:

1. Replace the fixed radial capture test with an opening signed-distance or containment test, preferably detecting segment crossing between previous and current parcel position so fast parcels cannot tunnel across a narrow lip.
2. Prevent the molten surface from drawing across the aperture. Density may remain in the reconstruction arrays, but fragments or triangles inside the opening must not cap the void.
3. Decide how much recent molten history remains on the lip. Deposits immediately outside the boundary should persist; material inside the void should not diffuse back onto the surface as if supported by ground.

For the bounded experiment, parcels should be counted as captured and removed when they cross the irregular mouth. A short dark throat below will make that disappearance spatially credible. A later experiment can decide whether a separate falling stream is artistically necessary.

## 4. Shared-library implications

No change is required in `world.js`, camera orchestration, controls infrastructure or either topology generator.

The proven membrane contract—`height`, `gradient`, `createGeometry` and `deform`—is worth preserving for the outer terrain. A throat-capable membrane would need a small extension, not a replacement framework:

- an irregular opening function such as `openingDistance(x, z)` or `containsSurface(x, z)`;
- local throat geometry creation or access;
- geometry rebuilding when throat controls change;
- a reliable gradient for the altered top surface, analytic where practical or central-difference sampled locally;
- a way for the molten layer to use the same opening boundary as the membrane and parcel capture.

The signed-off `createGaussianMembrane()` should remain untouched in behaviour. During the experiment, a sibling `createErodedThroatMembrane()` can implement the same established interface plus the opening-specific capabilities. It should be promoted into the general library only if the experiment succeeds.

`createSluiceGateLayer()` would need a narrow optional path: use a supplied opening predicate instead of its fixed `receiverRadius`, omit the circular receiver cap, and apply the opening mask to molten rendering. Defaults must retain the current reference behaviour.

The flow-driven part of the throat also needs read-only access to plausible gate-release approach descriptors: gate angle, inward outlet bearing and a stable weighting or identity. This should not create a second gate model. The smallest safe option is to derive the historical approach bearings from the same deterministic gate-layout data already used by the physical layer, either through a small shared layout helper or a read-only result returned by the layer. The throat may consume those descriptors when its static geometry is built; it must never emit parcels, release reservoir material or call gate operations.

## 5. Preserving the Gaussian well

Yes. The local transition can be blended to zero outside a selected patch radius, leaving the existing Gaussian exactly unchanged across most of the 24 × 24 territory.

The mouth contour should be deterministic and low-frequency, but deterministic must not mean arbitrary. It should combine two intelligible influences.

### Background erosion

Background erosion describes accumulated weathering, stress and material failure over a long timescale. It supplies the general non-circular character:

- begin with a displaced or mildly elliptical base opening;
- vary its radius using two or three low angular harmonics with a fixed seed;
- use those harmonics to produce broad recession, shelves and unequal steepness rather than fine noise;
- blend their height influence smoothly into the unmodified Gaussian across an outer transition band;
- keep amplitudes bounded so this component cannot dissolve into arbitrary damage.

### Flow-driven erosion

Flow-driven erosion supplies a small number of dominant channels or recessed sectors. Their bearings should be derived from plausible historical approaches of gate-released molten material. The current releases are examples of the repeated process that produced the throat, not cutting tools operating on the live mesh.

For each selected historical approach, use a broad angular kernel extending outward from the mouth into the local transition patch. Its orientation should come from the corresponding gate's inward release bearing, including the bounded outlet bias that gives the existing process its obliqueness. Its recession and depth may be weighted deterministically, but the channel must remain broad enough to read as accumulated wear rather than a traced copy of one packet path.

Only a subset of approaches should dominate. Equal cuts for every gate would merely replace one radial regularity with another. The background field and flow imprints should combine smoothly, with the resulting contour used consistently by the opening, lip mesh, capture predicate and molten mask.

This changes large-scale geometry rather than decorating a circle with high-frequency noise. The lip can contain long shelves, recessed sectors and one or two dominant cuts while still reading as one throat.

The historical geometry should be rebuilt only when its history seed, throat controls or relevant gate layout is deliberately changed—not during normal releases. Dynamically eroding the mesh from live molten density would add feedback, geometry updates and difficult causality before the static form has proved worthwhile. If a history-imprinted throat succeeds, genuine time-dependent erosion can be assessed later.

### Transport remains gated

The erosion field changes only the local terrain encountered after release. It must not participate in source emission, reservoir capture, gate selection or release scheduling. The causal sequence remains:

    incoming streams
        → shared reservoir
        → selected gates
        → released molten material
        → gravity well
        → eroded throat

No channel may begin at the reservoir, extract stored parcels or create a downhill bypass around a closed gate. The transition patch should remain well inside the current gate radii, and the existing gate machinery should continue to be the sole route by which reservoir material enters the well. Flow-driven channels may influence a released parcel through the membrane gradient only after that parcel has passed its gate; that is the intended terrain interaction, not leakage.

## 6. Plausible Three.js approaches

| Approach | Strengths | Limitations | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Composite heightfield only | Minimal change; existing force, UV, diagnostic and normal machinery continue to work | Cannot create an aperture, vertical wall or constriction; remains a deformed bowl | Useful preliminary profile test, but does not answer the throat question |
| Local irregular opening plus separate throat mesh | Preserves the outer Gaussian; produces a real void and descending wall; permits exact large-scale contour control | Requires one shared opening mask and careful lip/seam normals | Best bounded experiment |
| Rebuild the whole territory as custom topology | Can make one continuous mesh with exact lip topology and shared normals | Risks the signed-off outer surface, UV mapping and diagnostic behaviour; much larger scope | Unnecessary unless the local hybrid exposes irreparable seams |
| Shader displacement or opacity illusion only | Fast to prototype and easy to animate | CPU physics, normals, depth and visible form can disagree; repeats the archive's coordinate-conflation failure | Reject as the primary solution |
| CSG subtraction, voxel field or marching cubes | Supports undercuts, cavities and fully volumetric erosion | New dependencies or substantial machinery; difficult UVs and live controls; disproportionate for the question | Defer |

### Recommended hybrid geometry

Keep the outer grid and its Gaussian deformation. Within a compact patch, remove or discard top-surface coverage inside the same deterministic opening contour used by the simulation. Construct a local indexed `BufferGeometry` from concentric irregular rings:

1. an outer transition ring that meets the unchanged Gaussian;
2. several surface rings carrying the low-frequency background recession and the few gate-derived flow channels;
3. an explicit irregular mouth ring;
4. descending wall rings that taper toward a smaller, slightly displaced lower aperture;
5. darkness below, initially without a visible bottom cap.

The top transition and throat wall are genuine geometry. A shader discard may be acceptable only as the bounded method for preventing the unchanged outer grid from writing colour and depth inside the aperture; it must use exactly the same contour and must not impersonate the throat itself. If the grid boundary proves visibly coarse, rebuild only the small central patch rather than the whole territory.

## 7. Useful artistic controls

The initial control surface should stay small:

- **Mouth scale** — overall size of the opening without changing the broad Gaussian well.
- **Background erosion** — strength of displacement, ellipticity, low-frequency recession and shelves.
- **Flow imprint** — strength and breadth of the few gate-derived historical channels, without changing current gate release.
- **Throat constriction** — ratio between mouth size and the lower aperture.
- **Throat depth** — visible vertical extent before darkness.
- **History seed** — a reseed action for background history and deterministic dominance weighting, not a continuously varying noise frequency.

Existing depression depth, width and gate-layout controls remain important. Gate count and layout seed may change the available historical approach bearings, but the **Flow imprint** control must not alter gate state or release throughput. A separate centre-offset slider pair is probably excessive initially; a bounded, seed-derived displacement can be included in background erosion. Shelf strength should be added only if the first profiles demonstrate that it represents a distinct artistic question.

## 8. Normals, lighting, depth and cameras

### Normals and seams

The outer membrane can retain its current recomputed normals. The local top patch needs either shared seam vertices or deliberately matched boundary normals so the transition radius does not appear as a circular join. The lip may use duplicated vertices where a real geological crease is desired; otherwise averaging surface and upper-wall normals will make it look inflated.

The throat wall should have inward-facing normals and sufficient vertical/radial subdivision to avoid faceting. Geometry must be rebuilt, bounds recomputed and normals recalculated when controls change. The lower-resolution diagnostic mesh needs an explicit throat representation; stretching the existing wireframe across the void would give false evidence.

### Lighting and depth

The current key and rim lights should be tested before adding illumination. A successful throat ought to be readable from form, occlusion and reflected molten light associations, not a decorative glow. The top surface and molten overlay must write no depth inside the aperture, or the wall will appear capped. The present circular receiver should be removed or moved well below the constriction as an optional darkness terminator.

Transparent molten rendering will make ordering particularly sensitive around the lip. The v0.5a foreground-order lesson applies: depth relationships should be geometric and stable under orbit, not corrected with view-dependent draw tricks.

### Cameras

Elevated remains essential for judging contour, channel recession and whether the opening is genuinely non-circular. Monumental is the harder and more important test for scale, lip silhouette, wall depth and accidental bowl-like readings. Its target may need to move slightly downward for the experiment, but a new camera system is unnecessary. Orbit testing from several azimuths is mandatory because asymmetry can look convincing from one direction and arbitrary from another.

## 9. Implications for Cosmos

Cosmos topology does not need and must not acquire knowledge of the throat. Its current manifestation, however, assumes `membrane.height(x, z)` exists everywhere and returns one Y value. Every conduit polyline and communication metric is rebuilt from that function.

A genuine opening creates an unresolved manifestation choice for routes crossing it:

- clip the visible route at the mouth and resume it on the far side;
- bridge the void, which risks returning to a floating foreground layer;
- map it onto throat walls, which requires a surface chart rather than a height function;
- allow it to continue into depth through a separate manifestation rule.

Whichever is later selected, the logical nodes, edges and route choice must remain unchanged. Constant manifested-speed metrics will also need to understand clipped or multi-surface route segments. The present v0.6a experiment should not solve this. It should first establish a stable physical throat against which a separate Cosmos-only manifestation study can be judged.

## 10. Smallest bounded v0.6a experiment

Create a new sibling **physical-only** study derived from the signed-off physical layer. Preserve all behaviour outside the central transition.

Implement only:

1. the existing broad Gaussian well;
2. one deterministic low-frequency background erosion field inside a compact transition radius;
3. a few dominant flow-driven channels derived from deterministic gate-release approach bearings;
4. a genuine masked opening and short tapering throat wall;
5. an opening-crossing capture test replacing the circular receiver test;
6. molten reconstruction clipped at the same opening boundary;
7. the six meaningful controls above, existing physical controls and the diagnostic wireframe;
8. Elevated, Monumental and free-orbit inspection.

Do not implement dynamic erosion, wall-bound lava, a falling-fluid phase, Cosmos manifestation, a lower crucible, the Tree, post-processing or new materials intended to rescue weak geometry.

### Questions the experiment must answer

- Does the broad Gaussian form remain recognisable from all useful views?
- Does the centre read as stressed and eroded at silhouette scale rather than as a noisy circle?
- Does background erosion create credible general asymmetry without reading as seeded noise?
- Do the dominant channels correspond plausibly to gate-release approach bearings without tracing exact current packet paths?
- Do current molten approaches appear to repeat the kind of physical process that historically wore the lip?
- Does material disappear through a place rather than vanish at a radius?
- Are shelves and constriction visible under the existing lighting?
- Do controls produce coherent families of throats rather than random damage?
- Do reservoir counts, gate phases, selected releases and peripheral losses remain unchanged apart from downstream terrain response?
- Can diagnostics confirm that no material enters the well without first being released by a gate?
- Are seams, normals, depth and molten clipping stable while orbiting?

### Technical acceptance boundary

The experiment is successful enough to continue if:

- the outer well and established sluice dynamics remain recognisable;
- background erosion produces coherent large-scale asymmetry rather than arbitrary noise;
- a small number of dominant cuts have defensible correspondence with deterministic gate-release approach directions;
- the opening is genuinely uncapped and capture follows the same irregular contour;
- reservoir storage, gate selection, release timing and parcel emission are unchanged;
- no new source, channel or terrain rule can bypass a closed gate or leak material from the reservoir;
- at least several history and gate-layout seeds read as eroded geological structures from both cameras.

It need not yet explain what molten material does below the mouth.

## Recommendation

Proceed later with the bounded hybrid experiment above, beginning with the physical layer alone. If it succeeds, conduct a separate Cosmos-only study of how independent routes manifest at an absent or vertical surface. Only after both are understood should the throat be reintroduced into the combined Universe/Cosmos scene.
