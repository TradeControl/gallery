# Casting Pit v0.5a review — Universe / Cosmos Overlay

14 September 2026

## Review status

The combined experiment is technically operational and ready for human artistic review. This document records the initial implementation inspection; it does **not** declare the artistic proposition successful.

The experiment is located at:

    src/casting-pit/universe-cosmos-v0.5a/

The signed-off `physical-layer` and `information-layer` experiments remain separate and runnable.

## How the systems were combined

v0.5a creates one Three.js world, one Gaussian membrane and one shared parameter object. The physical and information factories are then mounted as independent systems into that world.

The Universe owns the visible environment: dark membrane material, receiver, lights, exposure and diagnostic wireframe. Cosmos is mounted without its duplicate membrane, receiver or lighting rig. Its topology, rendered conduits, ember substrate and communication signals are otherwise the signed-off v0.4b mechanisms.

Both systems register their existing frame callbacks with the same world loop. The molten simulation continues to integrate fixed physical steps; Cosmic signals continue to advance by manifested route distance. Neither callback calls or steers the other.

## Technical changes required

Three narrow composition seams were added to the established layer factories:

- an optional control prefix, allowing both legacy control sets to use unique HTML identifiers;
- an option for Cosmos not to bind environment controls owned by the combined root;
- a visibility method on each layer for Universe-only, Cosmos-only and combined inspection.

All three additions default to the original standalone behaviour. The standalone reference builds still pass.

The shared depth and width controls update both layers' manifested geometry. Other controls remain owned by their respective systems. Visibility changes do not pause or reset either simulation, so switching modes compares the same evolving state rather than three separately started scenes.

## Deliberately preserved

- Universe retains the shared rim reservoir, independent stream and gate counts, phased invisible sluice gates, packet storage and release, neighbour interaction, gravity and molten reconstruction.
- Cosmos retains its pit-independent seeded hierarchy, rough multi-tier conduits, territorial sampling, surface manifestation and comet-like communication droplets.
- Molten packets remain slow, accumulative and gravity-responsive.
- Communication remains route-bound and advances at constant manifested spatial speed, independent of slope and gravity.
- The established colour, material and emission treatments were not retuned to force compatibility.
- No throat, Tree, ERP semantics, fog, bloom or new post-processing layer was introduced.

## Shared-membrane default conflict

The signed-off studies do not use the same membrane default. Universe uses depth `5.5` and width `3.8`; Cosmos uses depth `7.1` and width `3.7`.

v0.5a opens at the Universe values. The reason is causal rather than cosmetic: the membrane belongs to the physical setting and its gradient directly governs the molten dynamics. Cosmos is explicitly intended to manifest across whatever spatial deformation it encounters without changing topology. The v0.4b graph defaults—including seed `2`, subdivision depth `7`, district footprint `1.7` and territory Z `4`—remain unchanged. Surface binding is the one compositional exception: it is fixed to `1.0` by default after the first orbiting review exposed routes floating toward the viewer at `0.82`.

This is an unavoidable difference from the standalone information page and should be part of human inspection. The shared membrane sliders make stronger deformation immediately available without regenerating either topology.

## Initial visual observations

### Elevated

Elevated gives the clearest account of coexistence. The Cosmic network remains broad enough to establish surrounding territory, while the molten rim and inward releases state the physical event. The white-hot communication heads remain identifiable against both the persistent gold substrate and the red-black molten bodies.

The overlap is not polite. Broad molten forms obscure substantial local circuitry, especially near the rim and well. This is useful evidence for the next decision because it exposes exactly the compositional pressure anticipated by v0.4b rather than concealing it.

### Monumental

Monumental strengthens the physical weight of individual molten releases. It also makes the behavioural contrast easier to read when a bright signal traverses a nearby conduit while a molten body descends. The lower angle sacrifices some overview: foreground material and the membrane edge hide more of the fine Cosmic hierarchy, while the long conduits do help imply a world beyond the frame.

### Layer relationship

Both systems read as inhabitants of the same depression because their geometry responds to one membrane. They remain distinguishable through persistence and movement: molten bodies widen, merge and accumulate; compact signals travel decisively along persistent routes.

The first overlay inherited the signed-off Cosmic surface binding of `0.82`. Orbiting exposed a structural compositing error: because route height was interpolated toward a flat plane, circuitry on steep slopes genuinely occupied space above the membrane and could move into the perceptual foreground as the camera changed.

The combined experiment now defaults to full binding `1.0`, scales the small above-surface lift of Cosmic marks to `45%`, and renders molten coverage after the information objects. These measures keep routes attached closely to the common membrane and give matter stable foreground precedence where the layers overlap. The information reference retains its original `0.82` setting and lift.

Live inspection orbited the Elevated scene through several materially different horizontal angles and one low Monumental angle. The former foreground inversion did not recur: routes stayed conformed to the membrane, molten bodies remained in front at intersections, and signals stayed visible on uncovered conduits. No browser warnings or errors were produced.

## Unexpected successes

- The information droplets survive the red-orange molten field better than expected at the existing brightness `0.85` because their compact pale cores and rapid directed motion differ strongly from the slower material.
- The same warm luminous family makes the systems feel materially related without erasing the behavioural distinction.
- Switching layers without resetting them is a particularly effective diagnostic. Returning to Combined reveals continuity rather than a staged comparison.
- The shared depression is immediately legible as one spatial event even though it has no influence on Cosmic topology.

## Conflicts and failures exposed

- Both systems demand luminous attention in dense regions. The result can become tangled rather than hierarchical where molten releases cover fine districts.
- The molten reconstruction can conceal exactly the near-rim and well circuitry that is most visibly deformed.
- Lowering surface binding below `1.0` deliberately reproduces the original floating-layer problem and should now be treated as a topology diagnostic rather than a combined artistic state.
- The full dual control panel is long. Collapsible system sections keep it usable for diagnosis, but it is an instrument rather than a future presentation interface.
- After an extended default run, the physical simulation reached its existing `700 / 700` parcel ceiling. At that sample the shared rim retained 138 parcels. This is a known high-inflow limitation of the signed-off physical system, not a new Cosmos interaction, but it can eventually starve new peripheral emission during long inspections.

## Behavioural observations

The systems continue to demonstrate different laws in the same scene. Cosmic signals neither bend toward the origin nor change their rate because of descent or ascent. Molten packets receive gravitational acceleration, interfere, accumulate at the rim and leave through phased releases. The strongest contrast occurs when signal heads cross a slope near a descending molten release: one event reads as traversal and the other as submission to the field.

No causal coupling has been introduced. Visual crossing does not create a network junction, and molten proximity does not alter signal paths.

## Performance observations

The production build succeeds. Browser inspection of Combined mode with the default 3,059-node, 4,222-connection graph and 110 signals produced no console warnings or errors.

During one late, parcel-saturated sample, the physical diagnostics reported approximately `3.50 ms` for the fixed simulation step and `4.40 ms` for molten reconstruction. Orbiting, mode changes and both camera presets remained responsive. These are indicative browser samples rather than a benchmark.

Vite reports only its existing advisory that the Three.js output chunk exceeds 500 kB.

## Useful human inspection states

1. Start in **Combined / Elevated** at the supplied defaults to judge the complete field.
2. Switch to **Universe only** without resetting, then return to Combined to identify what the molten reconstruction hides.
3. Switch to **Cosmos only** to confirm that hierarchy and signal routing remain intact on the shared membrane.
4. Compare **Combined / Monumental** for weight and near-slope behavioural contrast.
5. Keep **Surface binding** at `1.00` for normal combined inspection. Lower values remain available only to expose the difference between logical independence and spatial manifestation.
6. Raise shared depression depth toward the information study's `7.1` to compare a stronger well without changing network topology.
7. If a long run reaches the parcel ceiling, use **Reset process** before assessing peripheral arrival and reservoir rhythm.

## Questions for human review

1. Does the shared warm luminosity create one reality, or collapse Universe and Cosmos into visual competition?
2. Does full surface conformance and stable molten precedence resolve the orbiting problem without burying too much information?
3. Are the communication droplets sufficiently legible during the heaviest molten events?
4. Does occluded circuitry imply underlying order, or merely waste the Cosmos work?
5. Which camera best demonstrates “one is falling; the other is going somewhere”?
6. Is the collision magnificent, hideous, or interesting enough to continue?

## Initial assessment

All technical acceptance criteria are met. The combined scene exposes real compositional and ontological tensions rather than resolving them prematurely. That makes v0.5a a useful experiment, but the artistic proposition remains explicitly pending human review.
