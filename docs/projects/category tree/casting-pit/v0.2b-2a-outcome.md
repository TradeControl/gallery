# Casting Pit v0.2b-2a outcome — Molten Reconstruction Refinement

6 September 2026

## Scope

This was a bounded refinement of `src/casting-pit/molten/`, concentrating on:

- width variation;
- internal motion within reconstructed pools;
- less ribbon-like silhouettes.

No throat, extra visual layer, new source logic or packet-dynamics change was introduced. The earlier `ribbons/`, `parcels/` and `variable-packets/` experiments remain unchanged.

## Reconstruction changes

### Flow-supported field advection

The 224 × 224 reconstruction field now stores two additional scalar arrays containing density-weighted packet velocity in membrane material-space X and Z.

Packets deposit this local motion alongside density and heat. During each 30 Hz reconstruction update, velocity is recovered by dividing accumulated flow by local density. Density and heat are then sampled a short distance upstream using a bounded semi-Lagrangian backtrace before the existing spreading, cooling and decay are applied.

The advection factor is deliberately restrained. It does not create a second flow simulation and cannot steer packets. It allows recent packet-supported motion to continue through reconstructed material between deposits, particularly inside broad accumulations where the previous field merely expanded and faded in place.

The deposited flow field is itself spread locally and decays slightly faster than density. Old pools therefore lose directional memory rather than moving indefinitely after packet support has gone.

### Variable deposition footprint

The former fixed two-texel brush has been replaced by precomputed, normalised brushes with radii from two to four texels.

Footprint width now responds to:

- packet speed: slower material spreads more broadly;
- current reconstructed density: accumulation widens locally;
- the packet's existing mass-variation sample: individual packet bodies are not identical;
- bounded material-space variation: stable traffic does not produce perfectly parallel edges.

Brush weights are normalised back to the original two-texel brush total. A wider footprint redistributes the same deposited quantity rather than silently manufacturing more material.

No additional random sample is consumed. The footprint reuses the mass-variation value already drawn for each packet, preserving the previous seeded packet sequence exactly.

### Silhouette variation

The shader now varies its reconstruction threshold modestly with two scales of material-space noise. This affects only the molten boundary and makes thin and cooling margins recede unevenly instead of forming smooth rails.

This remains part of the existing molten material. No new mesh, post-processing pass, particle effect or decorative overlay was added.

## Visual observations

The refined result was inspected at 2, 6 and 12 streams, with seeds 1 and 2, default and low gravity, default and long persistence, packet diagnostics, and both Elevated and Monumental cameras.

### Width and silhouette

Two-stream tests remain the hardest case because half of the fixed 48-packet input can occupy one coherent lane. The captured stream now tapers and changes width through the slope rather than retaining a uniformly extruded profile. The tangential population reconstructs into a substantially broader body where its packets slow and accumulate.

The improvement is stronger with 6 and 12 streams. Crossings create flares, downstream bodies contract again, and dense confluences develop asymmetrical lobes. At 12 streams the previous impression of several similarly sized orange ribbons is replaced by a hierarchy of narrow arrivals, broad merged regions and locally pooled material.

Material-space boundary variation removes the most regular parallel edges without turning the result into random spray. The silhouette remains continuous and packet-supported.

Some coherent lanes can still appear conduit-like, especially from a low camera when a stream is viewed edge-on. They now read closer to viscous molten tongues than prescribed ribbons, but this limitation has not disappeared completely.

### Internal pool motion

At gravity `1.6`, persistence `8`, six streams and seed 2, the packet population again reached its 500-packet cap and reproduced the expected outward-stream failure. The reconstructed accumulations developed directional feeding and draining lobes instead of only growing symmetrically around stationary deposits.

Density and heat travel in the direction supported by local packet velocity, while stale regions cool and lose that directional memory. Broad pools therefore change outline internally and retain visible inlet/outlet relationships. The effect is restrained enough that the field remains aligned with the diagnostic packets rather than sliding away as an independent texture.

Severe congestion is still severe congestion. The refinement gives it more internal structure but does not disguise a saturated packet population as an automatically successful pool.

### Emergent dynamics

The source generator, constant total emission, surface forces, neighbour interactions, integration, capture and loss tests are unchanged. Diagnostic overlays at 12 streams showed current packets and velocity vectors lying within the advected molten histories, including around the central confluence.

Seed-dependent capture and escape remained intact. The low-gravity seed-2 run retained five capturing populations and the outward population's losses. The reconstruction changes how this evidence is embodied, not the evidence itself.

### Stream count

The Number of Streams control remains meaningful:

- 2 streams produce a few large bodies with strong individual histories;
- 6 streams produce distinct tongues, confluences and off-centre reservoirs;
- 12 streams produce fragmented arrivals which repeatedly combine into wider shared material.

Because total emission is unchanged, the visible distinction continues to concern organisation and fragmentation rather than quantity.

## Performance

The added velocity fields and bilinear backtracing increase reconstruction work but remain interactive. Observed reconstruction updates were approximately **1.8–3.1 ms**. Sampled complete fixed steps were typically **1.6–5.7 ms**, including heavily populated scenes; the low-gravity case remained responsive at 500 packets.

The browser reported no errors or warnings. The production build succeeds with the existing Vite advisory concerning the Three.js bundle size.

## Assessment

### Did width variation improve the material reading?

Yes. Speed-, density- and footprint-sensitive deposition gives meaningful narrowing, widening and confluence. Normalised brushes ensure that width is not merely increased by adding material. Boundary variation removes much of the mechanically parallel silhouette.

### Do reconstructed pools now contain useful internal motion?

Yes, within the limits of this reconstruction model. Their density and heat are advected by packet-supported local velocity, so pools show feeding, directional rearrangement and draining rather than static expansion. This is material history in motion, not a replacement fluid solver.

### Has the ribbon problem been solved?

Substantially reduced, not eliminated. Multi-stream encounters and pools read convincingly as shared molten material. An isolated, highly coherent lane can still resemble a conduit from some viewpoints. Further improvement would probably require a more sophisticated free-boundary or shallow-fluid treatment, which would exceed the deliberately small reconstruction model at this stage.

### Were the emergent packet dynamics preserved?

Yes. The deterministic random sequence and every trajectory-affecting calculation remain unchanged. All new calculations consume packet position, velocity, mass and reconstructed density only for deposition and rendering.

## Remaining limitations

- Coherent isolated streams can retain a conduit-like reading from low angles.
- Semi-Lagrangian field advection is deliberately dissipative and does not model pressure or conservation inside a true fluid.
- Material-space width variation is stable rather than advected as crust.
- Saturated packet congestion still produces oversized bodies; it is now easier to read, not solved.
- The unresolved receiver remains an abrupt central termination, intentionally deferred.

## Recommendation

**B. Proceed to v0.3 throat / central transition.**

The molten system is now sufficiently credible for this scale of reconstruction. Width responds to behaviour, pools possess packet-supported internal motion, crossings form shared material, and the worst ribbon regularity has been reduced without changing the dynamics. The remaining abruptness is increasingly concentrated at the central receiver, while further free-surface refinement would require disproportionate simulation machinery for uncertain benefit.
