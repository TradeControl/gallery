# Casting Pit v0.3 outcome — Peripheral Catchments and Phased Sluice Gates

6 September 2026

## Principal question

Can phased industrial capture and release make the molten system read as a deliberate casting process while preserving its emergent physical behaviour?

Yes, with an important visual correction after review. The new intermediate system changes the work's causality: material now arrives from beyond the territory, accumulates at invisible control boundaries, and enters the gravity well in timed surges. The downstream trajectories remain emergent.

## Experiment

The new sibling study is located at:

    src/casting-pit/sluice-gate/

It was derived from the current molten reconstruction. `molten/` and all earlier experiments remain unchanged. No central throat, Tree, circuitry, terrain treatment, post-processing or full fluid solver was introduced.

## Peripheral feed architecture

The former artwork-facing annular source population was replaced by independently configurable peripheral feeds and gates. The corrected deterministic generator creates 6–18 incoming streams and 2–8 unevenly placed gates. Streams and gates are spatially independent: all streams contribute to one shared annular reservoir around the well.

Feeds begin at radii `12.1–13.6`, at or beyond the visible 24 × 24 membrane. Gate radii vary from `5.35–6.15`. Angular position varies within deliberately irregular sectors so the catchments retain surrounding coverage without becoming evenly spaced.

Each feed is launched towards the outer catchment region of its assigned gate with a bounded angular error of approximately ±0.12 radians. Streams assigned to the same gate begin across a broad angular fan rather than sharing one origin. Mass varies from `0.90–1.16`, and cadence from `0.84–1.16` before normalisation. Total peripheral inflow is divided across streams.

There are no peripheral Bézier paths, splines or waypoints. After launch, packets are subject to the existing surface field, drag, momentum and neighbour interactions. Their initially non-radial placement and approach variation produces lateral curvature and irregular arrival rather than visible emitters arranged around the well.

## Catchments and physical storage

Each gate has a catchment immediately outside its aperture. When a packet enters a closed, opening or closing catchment, the gate takes ownership of that same packet object:

- downstream force and neighbour integration pause for the held packet;
- the packet receives a stable storage slot in a broad irregular fan behind the barrier;
- it continues to deposit cooler molten density, so stored material becomes a visible reservoir;
- its age is paused so storage is not faked by repeatedly destroying and respawning material;
- the gate's live stored count reports the actual packet array length.

The storage fan is a bounded substitute for rigid-wall and basin collision mechanics, which the current particle model does not possess. It is local to the industrial catchment and does not affect downstream trajectories.

The first pass rendered a restrained bar and two posts at each gate. Review showed that these literal objects competed with the molten work and over-specified the industrial metaphor. Gate geometry is now entirely hidden in the artistic view. Capture, storage and release remain physical process events, while the control panel's live state and stored count provide optional diagnostic evidence.

## Phased operation

Every gate shares an adjustable base cycle but receives a deterministic phase base from gate index and seed. **Phase spread** scales those offsets from synchronized operation to widely separated release windows.

Each cycle comprises:

1. **filling** behind the closed control boundary;
2. **opening**, once the release threshold has been reached in the scheduled window;
3. **releasing** through the raised aperture;
4. **closing** before storage resumes.

The opening is latched for the remainder of its current window so a gate does not chatter around the threshold. State and live storage count are shown in the diagnostic list.

The first implementation could release only 92% of its average cycle intake. Multi-cycle testing exposed this as a genuine throughput defect: every reservoir would grow eventually. Release capacity was corrected to approximately 118% of average cycle intake. This allows a scheduled opening to drain a reservoir while preserving visible storage between cycles.

The default peripheral inflow was reduced from the previous continuous experiment's 48 to **36 packets per second**. The 48 setting remains available as a congestion stress test, but was too close to the 500-packet cap for a storage-based default process.

## Release and downstream ownership

Opening gates release the original stored packets, not visual substitutes. A packet is placed just inside the invisible admission boundary and receives a single exit velocity.

The first pass placed releases too far from the strong part of the gravity field and allowed an outlet bias of approximately ±0.55 radians. Although individually attractive, the streams no longer reliably converged. The corrected system moves the admission radii inward to `5.35–6.15`, narrows outlet bias to approximately ±0.34 radians, and raises the one-time release speed to approximately `1.75–2.45`. This recovers decisive inward travel while retaining seeded obliqueness and variation.

After the exit impulse there is no gate force, source-specific correction or target seeking. The inherited gravity gradient, momentum, drag, pressure/separation, alignment/viscosity, cohesion and spatial hash exclusively determine downstream motion. Diagnostic vectors showed released surges curving, spreading, meeting other releases and amalgamating near the depression.

## Controls

The gate system exposes seven perceptible questions:

- **Number of gates:** 2–8, default 4;
- **Number of streams:** 6–18, default 12;
- **Peripheral inflow:** 12–96 packets/second, default 48;
- **Lava velocity:** 0.5–3 world units/second, default 1.4;
- **Gate cycle:** 5–20 seconds, default 11;
- **Phase spread:** 0–1, default 0.78;
- **Opening duration:** 1–5 seconds, default 2.5;
- **Release threshold:** 4–40 packets, default 12.

Existing membrane, downstream-physics, molten-field, camera and diagnostic controls remain. Reset deterministically replays the same layout and timing; Reseed creates a different reproducible layout, phasing and outlet bias.

Useful observed ranges were:

- 4–6 gates for a legible balance between individual reservoirs and overlapping releases;
- cycles around 8–13 seconds for visible storage without prolonged starvation;
- phase spread around 0.65–1 for continually changing composition;
- opening durations around 1.5–3 seconds;
- inflow around 28–40 for sustainable operation at the current 500-packet bound;
- thresholds around 8–18, depending on gate count and inflow.

## Visual tests

### Default five-gate process

At 5 gates, inflow `36`, cycle `11`, phase spread `0.78`, opening `2.5` and threshold `12`, several complete cycles remained below the parcel cap. A representative late-cycle sample contained 449 packets: one gate had drained to zero while the other four stored 22, 37, 56 and 58 packets. Five seconds later the total had fallen to 421 and a different phase composition was developing.

Snapshots repeatedly showed filling, opening, releasing and closing states at the same time. One side of the installation could be dormant behind a closed reservoir while another sent a broad surge down the slope. The image no longer behaved like one global pulse.

### Few slow gates

Three gates with a 16-second cycle, full phase spread, a 3-second opening and threshold 15 produced large, clearly separated reservoirs and isolated dominant releases. Stored counts reached 77 and 149 while the third gate was releasing.

This setting makes the dam mechanism extremely clear, especially from the Monumental view, but at inflow 48 it reached the 500-packet cap and a sampled fixed step rose to about 11 ms. It is a useful stress or dramatic setting, not a recommended default.

### More fast gates

Seven gates with a 6-second cycle, phase spread `0.82`, 1.5-second openings and threshold 6 produced smaller recurrent reservoirs and overlapping releases. With seed 2 and inflow 36, a representative sample contained 377 packets and simultaneously showed filling, opening and releasing gates. Stored populations ranged from 6 to 27.

Cross-source interactions were substantially greater in this regime—approximately 2,000–2,400 pairs in sampled frames—and the inner molten body changed dominance frequently. The Elevated view made the overall capture system clearest.

### Multiple seeds and views

Seeds 1 and 2 produced materially different irregular gate spacing, feed approaches, phase order and biased outlet directions while retaining the same fill/release/converge proposition.

The Elevated view best explains the complete sequence from edge arrival to catchment to central convergence. The Monumental view gives the individual reservoirs and descending streams greater weight, though the near membrane edge can obscure parts of the inner flow.

## Reservoir evidence

The reservoirs genuinely store packets. Their live counts rise while closed, decrease during scheduled release, and can reach zero. The molten history does persist briefly after drainage—as intended by the reconstruction field—but diagnostic mode distinguishes that cooling residue from the absence of stored particles.

Packet diagnostics showed dense zero-velocity populations immediately outside closed gates. During release, packets appeared inside the raised aperture with visible exit vectors, then acquired curved and interacting downstream histories. The system therefore does not merely switch a molten texture on and off.

## Artistic assessment

The industrial middle layer materially strengthens the casting reading. The invisible gate logic establishes agency and timing through behaviour: outside material arrives comparatively naturally; it pauses and accumulates; then it is admitted as a surge for the gravity well to shape.

Phasing supplies continuing complexity even though molten populations amalgamate. Reservoirs filling while others drain create changing visual dominance, transient collisions and new confluences without artificially preserving source colours.

The result retains the successful dull, variable-width molten material from v0.2b-2a. Catchment pools are particularly convincing because their width and heat respond to stored packet density, while released bodies narrow, flare and merge under the existing reconstruction.

## Performance

At the default and fast-gate settings the experiment remained interactive. Reconstruction updates were typically about **3–6 ms** and sampled complete fixed steps about **3–11 ms**, with the upper values occurring near the 500-packet cap. No browser errors or warnings were reported.

The production build succeeds. Vite continues to issue only the existing advisory that the Three.js bundle exceeds 500 kB.

## Weaknesses

- Catchment storage uses stable packet slots rather than rigid-wall collision, so reservoir packing is an explicit apparatus abstraction.
- Incoming feeds can still look broad and smooth before reaching the gate; terrain-scale natural meandering remains limited by the flat outer membrane and absence of authored paths.
- Very slow cycles combined with high inflow saturate the packet pool and starve new peripheral emission.
- Seven simultaneous streams can still briefly suggest a ring of spokes, although irregular placement, phasing and biased outlet directions reduce this.
- The invisible capture boundary is deliberately inferred from behaviour rather than represented as resolved foundry architecture.
- The central receiver remains an abrupt termination and was intentionally not addressed.

## Acceptance assessment

1. **Natural peripheral arrival:** passed within the smooth-membrane limitation; feeds enter from or beyond the visible territory and are not arranged as inner radial emitters.
2. **Perceptible industrial capture:** passed through accumulation and timed release; literal gate hardware was removed after review.
3. **Closed-gate accumulation:** passed with packet-backed stored counts.
4. **Visible release and drainage:** passed; stored counts decrease to zero and the same packets emerge through raised apertures.
5. **Phased composition:** passed; all four states coexist and dominance changes over multiple cycles.
6. **Emergent downstream behaviour:** passed; only the aperture exit impulse is prescribed.
7. **Casting-process reading:** passed; storage and timed admission add deliberate industrial causality.
8. **Molten material character:** passed; the v0.2b-2a field and shader remain the rendering basis.

## Recommendation

**B. Proceed to v0.3 throat / central transition.**

The peripheral/gate proposition succeeds strongly enough to retain. Further gate work should wait for broader compositional review: the current functional architecture already proves genuine storage, phased release and emergent downstream convergence. The principal unresolved Casting Pit event is now what happens when the shared molten body reaches the centre.

## Post-review correction

Immediate review identified two compositional failures in the first v0.3 pass: the gate objects should not be visible, and the wide, distant release geometry had lost the strong convergence achieved in earlier molten experiments.

The rendered gate group is now hidden unconditionally. The storage and phase machinery is unchanged, so the gates remain genuine simulation controls rather than visible scenery. The admission boundary was moved modestly inward, outlet deviation was reduced, and the existing single exit impulse was strengthened. No downstream steering, target seeking or new visual layer was introduced.

Full-cycle visual checks with layout seeds 1 and 2 showed independently phased streams descending into and joining one shared central molten body. The two seeds retained materially different stream shapes and timings. Gate states and stored counts continued to fill and drain, confirming that restored convergence did not bypass the packet-backed catchments.

Further review identified a deeper modelling conflation: one generated stream still implied one gate. Feed count and gate count are now independent. The revised default uses **12 incoming streams and 4 gates**. Incoming channels cross the membrane towards distributed points on a shared rim; they are not assigned to gates. Changing either count regenerates the deterministic layout without coupling the other value.

Lava velocity is now an explicit control with a deliberately slower default of `1.4`, replacing generated incoming speeds of `2.35–3.0`. Small seeded variation remains around the chosen value. The same control sets the scale of gate-release impulses and immediately rescales packets already in motion, so its visual effect can be judged live rather than only after reset.

The shared rim is packet-backed rather than a decorative ring. Any incoming packet crossing its outer boundary is captured at its arrival angle, moves slowly around an irregular annular band, cools and deposits molten density there. A gate can release only nearby reservoir packets during its own phase; it cannot drain material remotely from the opposite side. The release threshold scales with gate count so a visible common reservoir forms before discharge begins.

This changes the causal sequence to **distributed streams → shared rim accumulation → independent phased gates → pit**. Visual tests showed crossings across the outer membrane, a continuous but uneven molten rim, and simultaneous filling and releasing gate states. At a late default-cycle sample the rim held 280 real packets while two of four gates were releasing. The parcel ceiling was raised from 500 to 700 to accommodate the additional explicit storage stage; the sampled fixed step remained below 7 ms.

### Post-review artistic observation — low gate count

Further live visual review found that a two-gate configuration produces a particularly strong composition.

With many peripheral streams feeding the shared rim but only two phased release points, the reservoir develops a clear visual identity and individual releases become substantial periodic lavafalls rather than components of continuous central traffic.

This creates a pronounced temporal rhythm:

**accumulation → release → lavafall → convergence → recovery**

Higher gate counts remain useful for continuous, complex confluence, but low gate counts produce fewer and more monumental events.

This is an artistic observation rather than a change to the underlying model. The gate count should therefore remain configurable rather than selecting a single canonical value at this stage.
