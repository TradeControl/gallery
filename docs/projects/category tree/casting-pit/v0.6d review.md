# Casting Pit v0.6d review — mouth-crossing continuity

**Date:** 27 September 2026
**Status:** design and diagnostic review; no implementation authorised by this document

## Assessment

The v0.6c screenshot contains two related but technically distinct visual effects:

1. a small **grey, rock-like form** at the apparent singularity;
2. a **dark aperture** surrounding and behind it.

The grey form is not a receiver, cap, rock or extra object placed at the centre of the well. It is a foreshortened section of the upper throat wall. Although that wall is geometrically arranged around the opening, the perspective camera projects part of it over the centre of the two-dimensional image. Because v0.6c gives the throat the same grey material as the membrane, the projected wall catches light while the open shaft behind it remains nearly black. The eye separates the lit fragment from its surroundings and interprets it as a buried solid object.

The dark aperture is the genuine negative space of the open bore. It remains even when the throat mesh is hidden because the terrain and molten overlay contain a real opening. Several molten approaches and sloping terrain sectors project towards that same region, making the remaining darkness small, irregular and strongly framed.

This distinction matters. Removing the grey throat-wall fragment would improve the image, but it would not by itself show where the molten material goes. Conversely, adding a molten trace without correcting the projected wall could make the trace appear to strike or pass behind a rock.

v0.6d should therefore address **mouth-level visual continuity** in two ordered stages:

- make the upper throat wall read as the inside of the terrain rather than a separate grey object;
- provide restrained causal evidence that released molten material crosses the mouth and continues into depth.

## Current established state

The following results should be retained.

### Physical transport

The established causal sequence is:

```text
incoming streams
    → shared reservoir
    → selected gates
    → released molten material
    → gravity well
    → opening capture
```

Streams, gates, reservoir storage, release timing, parcel interactions and molten reconstruction are functioning and remain the behavioural reference.

### Historical terrain

Background erosion and gate-derived flow erosion alter a deterministic historical mouth profile. They represent accumulated physical history, not erosion occurring during the running scene.

### Canonical well frame

v0.6b established one centre at world `(0, 0)` for:

- the Gaussian minimum;
- the opening predicate;
- historical flow cuts;
- the throat axis;
- parcel capture.

That correction is valid and must remain.

### Coherent open shaft

v0.6c replaced the short uneven throat ending with a level circular continuation extending beyond the camera's far plane. The terminal construction boundary is no longer visible. The shaft has no cap, floor or receiver.

This correction is also valid, even though it did not solve the visual anomaly.

## Why a rim modification appears at the centre

The throat is more than a modified edge. `createThroatGeometry()` constructs a complete annular wall descending from the eroded mouth into the shaft. At every depth it contains a ring of vertices around the canonical axis, with triangles connecting that ring to the next.

The upper part begins at approximately the eroded mouth radius and descends through the live `throatDepth` while approaching the live `throatConstriction`. It therefore slopes inward as well as downward.

In a perspective view, a screen pixel does not identify a fixed world `x,z` coordinate. It represents a ray from the camera through the scene. A ray aimed at the apparent centre can encounter the near or far side of an annular wall before it reaches the axis behind it.

Diagnostic ray intersections through the reported region struck throat geometry at approximately:

- radial distance `1.0–1.2` from the world centre;
- world height `-5.9` to `-6.4`.

Those values belong to the upper throat, not to a rock at `(0, 0)` and not to the remote terminal ring near `y = -80`.

The effect can therefore be summarised as:

```text
annular wall around the opening
    + inward/downward slope
    + oblique perspective projection
    + grey standard material receiving light
    + near-black shaft behind it
    = apparent grey object at the image centre
```

No throat vertex needs to occupy the singularity for its projected image to cover it.

## Evidence from v0.6c diagnostics

The preceding experiments isolated the components as follows.

| Diagnostic | Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal ring moved beyond the far plane | Grey/dark form remained | It is not the lower mesh ending. |
| Throat temporarily coloured magenta | Magenta appeared in the form | Throat-wall geometry contributes to it. |
| Ray intersection inspection | First hits occurred on the upper throat | The relevant geometry is near the mouth. |
| Back faces removed | Form remained | It is not an accidentally exposed outer face. |
| Throat material matched to membrane | Form became grey but remained object-like | Material discontinuity was reduced, but lighting still separates the projected wall. |
| Constriction set to `1.0` | Form remained | Immediate funneling is not the sole cause. |
| Throat mesh hidden | A smaller dark irregularity remained | The real opening and its negative space are a second component. |
| Analytic collar added | A lit ring appeared | Filling the seam recreates the rejected teacup rim. |
| Surface resolution doubled | Form remained | It is not primarily grid resolution. |

The current screenshot is therefore not evidence that a stray mesh or old receiver survived. It is evidence that the current throat is geometrically present but visually under-explained.

## The v0.6d design question

The review question should be:

> What is the minimum causal visual evidence required for the upper throat to read as terrain continuing into depth, and for released molten material to read as crossing the mouth rather than terminating around a grey or black object?

The answer should not be another arbitrary structure placed at the centre. It should derive from two things that already exist:

- the physical terrain wall;
- actual molten parcels crossing the established opening predicate.

## Candidate approaches

### A. Depth-aware throat material only

Give the throat geometry an explicit depth coordinate. At the rim it matches the membrane material; below the rim it darkens smoothly and loses specular prominence before the wall projects into the singularity.

This would directly address the grey rock. It would also be physically reasonable: the upper attachment receives environmental light while the shaft rapidly becomes occluded.

It should not be implemented as a flat black replacement. A sudden transition would create another visible band. The material response should be continuous from the terrain into the throat.

**Advantages**

- minimal change;
- no new simulation;
- directly suppresses the lit grey fragment;
- preserves the existing throat geometry.

**Limitations**

- the black negative space remains;
- molten channels may still appear to terminate around an empty knot;
- it improves terrain continuity without proving molten continuity.

**Assessment:** necessary but probably insufficient.

### B. Short below-mouth molten trace only

When an already released parcel crosses the existing opening predicate, retain a short-lived visual record of that crossing below the mouth. It could be a small descending streak or elongated droplet aligned with the parcel's crossing velocity and the canonical shaft.

This is not a new source. It is a visual continuation of material that the simulation currently removes at capture.

The trace should:

- begin at the measured segment/opening intersection rather than at the origin;
- inherit source colour, temperature, mass and velocity where practical;
- descend only through the upper visible throat;
- fade or leave the visible region quickly;
- perform no deposition and exert no force;
- exist only after genuine gate-released material crosses the opening.

**Advantages**

- explains where material goes;
- retains gate and parcel causality;
- remains much smaller than a falling-fluid simulation.

**Limitations**

- the grey wall fragment may remain behind or beside it;
- isolated bright droplets could resemble sparks or decorative particles;
- excessive lifetime or count could fill the throat artificially.

**Assessment:** conceptually strong but incomplete on its own.

### C. Molten-derived interior illumination only

Crossing events could briefly illuminate the upper throat. Intensity would be derived from recently captured parcel mass and temperature, then decay when no material enters.

This illumination should affect only the throat interior. It must not introduce a permanently glowing well or a free-standing light source unrelated to transport.

**Advantages**

- makes the cavity respond to physical events;
- can reveal the wall as a continuous interior surface;
- provides temporal evidence of gate pulses.

**Limitations**

- light without visible motion may still leave destination ambiguous;
- a strong point light could recreate a glowing object at the centre;
- a permanent minimum intensity would become decorative rather than causal.

**Assessment:** useful support, not a complete explanation.

### D. Bounded hybrid — recommended

Combine:

1. a depth-aware throat material that prevents the upper wall becoming a detached grey fragment;
2. a short visual continuation for actual captured parcels;
3. restrained, rapidly decaying interior illumination derived from those same crossings.

The three components answer different parts of the problem:

- material treatment says **this is continuous terrain**;
- descending traces say **material passes through here**;
- illumination says **the cavity physically responds to that passage**.

The trace and illumination should be independently switchable in diagnostic mode so their contributions can be assessed rather than bundled into an inseparable effect.

**Assessment:** recommended for a bounded v0.6d experiment.

### E. Camera restriction

Restrict orbit controls to prevent near-axial views.

This could reduce the projection that creates the object-like reading, but it would conceal rather than resolve the ambiguity and would reduce the interactive character of the experiment.

**Assessment:** reject as the primary solution. Camera limits may later be chosen compositionally, but not as defect concealment.

### F. Redesign the opening and capture plane

Move, enlarge or reshape the opening, or change where parcels are captured.

This might enlarge the visible cavity or change its projection, but it risks breaking the proven correspondence between terrain, molten mask and capture. It could also alter wastage and the signed-off gate dynamics.

**Assessment:** defer unless the bounded hybrid fails.

## Recommended implementation boundary

The v0.6d experiment should be deliberately smaller than a below-mouth fluid simulation.

### Permitted

- add a depth value or colour factor to throat vertices;
- adjust only the throat material response using that depth;
- emit transient visual records from real opening-crossing events;
- aggregate those events into a decaying throat-illumination value;
- expose diagnostic switches for trace and illumination;
- tune trace lifetime, length and illumination decay within restrained limits.

### Not permitted

- create molten material independently inside the throat;
- alter reservoir, gate selection or release timing;
- change parcel forces before the mouth;
- allow traces to deposit back onto the surface;
- simulate wall adhesion, splashing, pooling or a continuous waterfall;
- add a cap, receiver, collar, rock or permanent glowing core;
- strengthen erosion to hide the issue;
- change Cosmos, the information layer or the Tree;
- use bloom or post-processing as a substitute for continuity.

## Suggested technical sequence

### Stage 1 — throat-material diagnostic

1. Add a normalised depth attribute to the throat geometry: `0` at the terrain attachment and increasing down the visible throat.
2. Preserve the membrane material at depth `0`.
3. Progressively reduce reflected light and colour value below the rim.
4. Compare the existing and depth-aware material with molten flow temporarily paused and active.
5. Confirm that the grey rock disappears without producing a black horizontal band or visible collar.

If this stage fails, do not add molten traces to conceal it. Reassess the upper throat projection first.

### Stage 2 — crossing-event record

1. At the existing swept `crossesOpening()` decision, calculate or retain an approximate crossing point along the parcel's final segment.
2. Record only parcels that have already been released by a selected gate.
3. Create a bounded transient with position, direction, source colour, temperature, mass and age.
4. Move it a short distance down the canonical shaft with a simple prescribed descent.
5. Remove it after a short lifetime or once it leaves the visible upper throat.

This record is a rendering continuation of captured material, not a new physics population.

### Stage 3 — event-derived illumination

1. Sum a bounded contribution from active crossing traces.
2. Apply it to the upper throat only.
3. Decay it rapidly and completely when crossing stops.
4. Prevent the illumination from forming a visible point source at the axis.
5. Compare traces-only, illumination-only and combined modes.

### Stage 4 — adversarial visual review

Test:

- no molten release;
- one gate releasing;
- several gates releasing;
- sparse and dense parcel traffic;
- minimum and maximum throat constriction;
- minimum, default and maximum erosion;
- elevated, monumental, aerial and free-orbit views;
- diagnostic modes on and off.

## Acceptance criteria

### Terrain reading

- No grey rock, detached wall fragment or lit solid appears at the singularity.
- The upper throat reads as the interior continuation of the membrane.
- The terrain-to-throat transition does not create a teacup rim, collar or horizontal band.
- The open shaft remains uncapped and aligned to the canonical well frame.

### Molten continuity

- During a release, material visibly crosses the mouth and continues into depth.
- The trace begins at the actual opening crossing rather than spawning at the centre.
- Trace frequency and colour correspond to captured gate-released parcels.
- When no parcels cross, all traces and molten-derived illumination disappear completely.
- The effect does not resemble unrelated sparks, rain or a permanent central flame.

### Causality

- Closed gates cannot generate throat traces or illumination.
- The reservoir remains the only route to release.
- No additional wastage path or source is introduced.
- Existing parcel capture counts remain consistent with the crossing events used for rendering.

### Visual robustness

- The singularity reads as an active cavity rather than a grey or black object in elevated, monumental, aerial and free-orbit views.
- The effect remains credible at low and high traffic densities.
- It remains understandable with diagnostic overlays disabled.
- It does not depend on restricting the camera to hide the problem.

### Preservation

- v0.6b coordinate alignment remains unchanged.
- v0.6c open-shaft geometry remains unchanged except for the new depth attribute.
- Historical erosion controls retain their meaning.
- Surface molten reconstruction before the mouth remains materially unchanged.
- Cosmos and Category Tree work remain out of scope.

## Recommendation

Approve **Approach D: bounded hybrid** in principle, implemented in the staged order above.

The grey rock should first be treated as a projected, over-lit piece of the upper throat wall. Only after that wall reads as terrain should actual crossing events be given a brief visible continuation and restrained illumination below the mouth.

This is a controlled expansion beyond the earlier prohibition on below-mouth molten representation. It does not authorise a new fluid simulation. It authorises only enough causal continuation to answer the specific perceptual failure that v0.6c has isolated.
