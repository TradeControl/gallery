# Casting Pit v0.2b-1a outcome — Adversarial Launch Tests

6 September 2026

## Question tested

Can the existing gravity-well and parcel-interaction model capture deliberately inconvenient launches, and do parcel encounters visibly change the result?

This remained a diagnostic experiment. The membrane, receiver, force model, spatial hash and fixed-step integration were retained. No steering, paths, channels, roughening, molten reconstruction or post-processing were introduced.

## Changes to the diagnostic

The six populations now have distinct, named launch cases:

| Colour | Case | Initial condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Radial control | Starts left of the well and travels substantially inward. |
| Blue | Tangential capture | Starts above the well and travels approximately perpendicular to its radial direction. |
| Yellow | Interceptor A | Starts upper-left and travels right. |
| Green | Interceptor B | Starts lower-right and travels up, producing an approximately 90-degree encounter with A on the slope. |
| Purple | High momentum | Starts to the right with greater mass and a much larger tangential velocity. |
| Cyan | Outward challenge | Starts lower-left with both velocity components initially directed away from the well. |

The panel identifies these cases and reports captured/lost totals for each. It also reports cross-source and interceptor-only neighbour pairs. An **Interceptors only** view makes the encounter test legible without changing the simulation. Velocity vectors remain available.

## Observations

### Default dynamics

At gravity `3.2`, drag `0.32` and interaction `1.4`, all six cases were repeatedly captured with no observed losses. A representative 12-second run recorded 51 radial, 45 tangential, 80 interceptor A, 60 interceptor B, 40 high-momentum and 50 outward captures, with 262 parcels still in flight.

The red control is the direct case. The blue population initially crosses the slope, bends into an orbit-like curve and is then drawn into the receiver. This is visibly different from aiming at the hole.

The purple population travels materially farther around the depression than the control. Its initial momentum remains evident before drag and the increasing surface gradient turn it inward. It is not merely a delayed radial path.

The cyan population initially moves away from the receiver, slows, reverses and is captured. This is the clearest qualitative demonstration that the field, rather than convenient source aim, determines the eventual destination.

Surface attachment remained stable throughout inspection. With all cases active the measured simulation time was approximately 2–4 ms per rendered update on this machine; no browser warnings or errors were reported.

### Deliberate interception

The earlier long-distance interceptor arrangements were rejected during development: their paths approached the same receiver but did not form a visually convincing encounter. Moving the two starts onto opposing sides of the local slope made the perpendicular initial velocities meet off-centre while still leaving all subsequent motion to the existing forces.

The isolated, seeded comparison distinguishes contact from coincidence:

- With interaction `0`, the yellow and green streams pass through the crossing, retain narrow independent paths and accumulate substantially. At six seconds approximately 100 parcels remained active and neither population had yet registered a capture.
- With interaction `1.4`, the encounter changes direction and coherence, disperses the two colours and drains them more rapidly. At the same observation interval 43 parcels remained active, with 29 yellow and 26 green parcels already captured.

The exact instantaneous neighbour count varies as packets pass the crossing, but the replayed visual and dwell-time differences remain. Interaction is therefore visually consequential, not merely detectable by a counter.

### Low and intermediate gravity

The previous apparent discrepancy is real and useful. Convergence is not universal across the slider.

At gravity `1.2`, drag `0.32` and interaction `1.4`, the population reached the 500-parcel cap. After roughly 22 seconds the radial and interceptor cases continued to capture, the tangential case had 95 captures and 3 losses, but the high-momentum case had 28 losses and no captures and the outward case had 43 losses and no captures. Weak gravity cannot dominate those hostile initial conditions before their lifetime or bounds expire.

At gravity `2.2`, the radial, tangential, interceptor and high-momentum cases captured without observed loss during a 14-second run. The outward case had neither captured nor escaped in that interval and the active population rose to 428, showing that this is a slow transitional regime rather than the same behaviour at a different speed.

The useful diagnostic range is consequently broad enough to expose distinct regimes:

- around `3.2` with drag `0.32`, every hostile case converges convincingly;
- around `2.2`, most cases converge but the outward challenge has a long dwell time;
- around `1.2`, high-momentum and outward launches fail clearly while easier cases still demonstrate capture;
- interaction `0` versus `1.4` produces an obvious interceptor control comparison.

These are observations, not newly tuned success thresholds. The existing dynamics coefficients were left unchanged.

## Failures and limitations

The diagnostic still looks like coloured marbles because parcels and source colours are intentionally exposed as instrumentation. It does not answer how a reconstructed continuous molten surface will look.

At low gravity, long-lived parcels fill the fixed pool, so captured/lost totals and the visual backlog must be read together. At intermediate gravity the outward case takes longer than a short review run to resolve. The interaction effect is clearest in the isolated comparison; six simultaneous populations partially obscure it.

The receiver is still a central sink. This experiment establishes capture by the surface field and consequential parcel interaction, not a physically complete drain or throat.

## Acceptance assessment

1. **Tangential capture:** passed; the blue path is progressively curved inward.
2. **High-momentum resistance then capture:** passed at the default setting; it takes a visibly different, longer route.
3. **Consequential intersection:** passed; the isolated on/off replay changes direction, coherence and dwell time.
4. **Emergent trajectories:** passed; only initial position and velocity differ and no post-launch guidance exists.
5. **Attachment and performance:** passed in the tested population range.
6. **Meaningful control range:** passed; default, transitional and failure regimes are all observable.

## Recommendation

**A. Proceed to v0.2b-2 molten reconstruction.**

The hostile tests provide genuine traction for the free-flow idea. The gravity well captures tangential, high-momentum and initially outward material at the default settings; the interceptor control demonstrates a visible interaction consequence; and low gravity produces intelligible failures rather than being silently tuned away. The next uncertainty is therefore reconstruction of a continuous molten appearance, not another redesign of the parcel dynamics.
