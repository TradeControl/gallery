# Casting Pit v0.6c — continued well throat outcome

**Date:** 17 September 2026
**Status:** static continuation implemented and verified; principal visual anomaly remains

## Outcome

v0.6c implemented the static throat continuation proposed in `v0.6b-outcome.md`. It successfully removes the former finite, uneven terminal ring from every renderable camera view and gives the throat a coherent circular shaft below its historically irregular mouth.

It does not remove the small dark form seen at the centre of steep and aerial views.

The diagnostic passes established that the remaining form is not the old receiver, a displaced well centre, the lower terminal ring, a back-face leak or a low-resolution opening-mask error. It is the visible negative space of the open bore, framed by the upper throat and by molten approaches projected across the surrounding Gaussian slopes. Moving the construction boundary farther away cannot remove the necessary darkness of an open shaft viewed along its axis.

v0.6c therefore succeeds as a geometry correction but fails its principal visual acceptance test. It has exposed a boundary in the current approach: below-mouth terrain geometry alone cannot make the opening read as active depth when the near-aerial composition presents it as a small dark aperture surrounded by converging molten channels.

## Implemented geometry

### Deep open continuation

The throat now consists of:

- the historically irregular upper attachment;
- eight controlled upper-throat intervals;
- a coherent circular shaft;
- forty-eight continuation intervals extending `72` world units below the controlled throat.

The resulting default mesh contains:

- 57 rings;
- 7,353 vertices;
- 14,336 triangles.

The continuation remains open. No cap, receiver or floor was added.

At the default settings its final ring lies at approximately `y = -79.98`. Its distance from the elevated camera is approximately `91.45` world units, beyond the camera's `80`-unit far plane. The construction boundary therefore cannot be rendered from that view. The same continuation remains beyond or effectively at the far limit of the permitted upper-hemisphere orbit range.

### Coherent depth profile

The irregular upper-rim heights now progressively converge on a shared vertical reference through the controlled throat depth. The final shaft rings are level rather than inheriting the approximately half-unit height variation formerly present at the bottom of v0.6b.

The radial profile is treated similarly:

- at the mouth it retains the complete background and flow-derived historical asymmetry;
- through the controlled throat it transitions towards the mean opening radius multiplied by the live throat-constriction value;
- at the shaft it is circular and remains constant for the continuation.

This preserves the eroded mouth without reproducing its lobed outline indefinitely down the bore.

### Canonical frame retained

The v0.6b canonical-frame correction remains intact. The Gaussian minimum, opening frame, flow-cut frame and authored throat axis remain centred on world `(0, 0)`.

### Terrain material continuity

The throat now uses the membrane's colour, metalness and roughness rather than a separate near-black material. It is rendered as the one-sided inward surface defined by the throat's winding. The upper throat therefore belongs materially to the surrounding terrain while ordinary lighting still allows the deep shaft to darken.

This reduces an unnecessary material discontinuity. It does not remove the central negative space.

No changes were made to:

## Diagnostic sequence

v0.6c required several explicit isolation tests because the first proposed remedy did not alter the image as expected.

### 1. Extended tapered continuation

The first continuation extended the throat by `18` units and narrowed it to a very small level aperture. The dark central form remained almost unchanged.

This disproved the initial assumption that the reviewed anomaly was simply the silhouette of the former lower terminal ring.

### 2. Unmistakable throat colour

The throat material was temporarily changed to bright magenta. Magenta geometry appeared at the central form, proving that throat-wall geometry participates in the image. The diagnostic colour was then removed.

This did not by itself identify which depth of the throat was visible.

### 3. Ray-level depth identification

Ray intersections through the affected screen region struck the upper throat at approximately:

- radius `1.0–1.2`;
- height `-5.9` to `-6.4`.

They did not first strike the remote continuation. This showed that the visible form was dominated by the mouth-level construction and its projection, not the end of the tube.

### 4. One-sided interior material

The double-sided throat material was replaced by the correctly wound inward-facing side only. The central form remained.

The anomaly was therefore not an outer or back face leaking through the opening.

### 5. Membrane material continuity

The throat was given the same material response as the surrounding membrane. This reduced the impression of an independently coloured component, but the dark aperture remained.

Material discontinuity was contributory, not causal.

### 6. Maximum throat constriction

The existing constriction control was tested at `1.0`, producing an unconstricted upper throat. The central form remained.

Immediate funneling was therefore not its sole cause.

### 7. Throat removed entirely

The throat mesh was temporarily hidden while the opening predicate, terrain and molten system remained active. A small dark form persisted at the same compositional location.

This was the decisive result. The image is not generated solely by the throat mesh. It is also the actual negative space revealed by the opening, bounded by the projected terrain and molten approaches.

The throat was restored after this test.

### 8. Analytic collar

A temporary annular collar was inserted between the coarse membrane cut and the exact opening contour. It replaced the dark teeth with a clearly lit ring and recreated the rejected teacup-rim reading.

The collar was removed.

### 9. Higher-resolution surface cut

The v0.6c membrane and molten meshes were temporarily increased from 180 to 360 subdivisions. This halved the grid-cell scale around the opening but did not remove the central form. It also increased geometry cost without answering the artistic problem.

The original 180-subdivision resolution was restored.

### 10. Circular shaft regularisation

The deepest visible throat profile was changed from the inherited lobed contour to a circular shaft. This removes an unnecessary continuation of surface erosion into geological depth and makes the geometry more coherent. The central aperture nevertheless remains visible in the aerial composition.

## Why the visual objective remains unmet

The previous outcome assumed the anomaly was an exposed construction boundary. v0.6c proves that this was only part of the interpretation.

The well is a deep Gaussian depression. Molten streams travel on its sloping surface towards an opening positioned several units below the surrounding plane. In a steep view, terrain and molten paths on different sides of the depression project towards the centre of the image. Between them, the viewer looks along the open throat and sees darkness.

That negative space is physically correct, but the current composition does not provide enough information below the mouth for it to read as depth. Surrounded by several red approaches, its small irregular silhouette instead reads as a discrete black object, knot or plug.

No finite adjustment to the unseen terminal ring can change that relationship. A longer shaft, a smaller final aperture and a circular continuation all leave the mouth-level negative space present. A cap would make the plug reading literal. A collar would make a rim. Removing the throat would leave the hole cut through the membrane.

The problem is now perceptual rather than primarily topological:

> The open bore is geometrically valid, but it lacks a visible continuity cue connecting the surface flow to depth.

## Verification

Nine combinations of minimum, default and maximum background erosion and flow imprint were checked numerically.

For every combination:

- the Gaussian, opening frame and throat axis remained at `(0, 0)`;
- the origin remained inside the opening;
- swept-segment opening capture remained active;
- all throat positions and normals were finite;
- the continuation ended in a level circular ring;
- no cap or alternative material source was introduced.

At the default state, the terminal radius is approximately `0.5214` world units and is constant around the final ring.

The final implementation was run in the browser with active molten flow and free orbit, including a near-aerial recreation of the reported view. No browser warnings or errors were produced. Indicative live readings remained approximately `1.8 ms` for the simulation step and `5.0 ms` for molten reconstruction with 618 active parcels. These are inspection samples rather than benchmarks.

## Recommended next step

Do not continue lengthening, tapering or subdividing the throat. Those variables have been separated from the remaining visual problem.

The next review should decide which new source of depth continuity is conceptually acceptable. Plausible directions are:

1. **A restrained below-mouth molten transition.** Released material could remain visible for a short distance after crossing the mouth, providing causal continuity without attempting a complete falling-fluid simulation.
2. **Interior illumination derived from released material.** Existing molten energy could light the upper throat briefly as material enters, allowing the cavity to read through response rather than a new decorative layer.
3. **A deliberate camera constraint.** Near-axial views could be limited if they fundamentally collapse the surface and throat into an object-like aperture, although this would reduce interactive freedom and should not be adopted merely to conceal a defect.
4. **A different mouth-level representation.** The physical opening and capture plane could be reconsidered together, but only through a new review because this risks altering the established transport relationship.

Options 1 and 2 cross the previous scope boundary and therefore require explicit conceptual approval before implementation. They should not be smuggled into further terrain work.

## Recommendation

Retain the v0.6b canonical frame and the v0.6c coherent open-shaft geometry as technically sound foundations. Do not describe v0.6c as an artistic success and do not proceed directly to layer synthesis.

The next task should be a short review of mouth-crossing continuity: determine the minimum physically meaningful visual evidence needed for released molten material to appear to enter the throat rather than terminate around a black object. That question must be settled before additional throat refinement can be evaluated fairly.
