# Casting Pit v0.2 outcome

5 September 2026

## Result

The v0.2 study answers its question positively: several distinct luminous flows can cross the established membrane, descend with its deformation, converge and visibly disappear at the current centre. Their paths and restrained local light make the gravity well substantially easier to understand with the diagnostic overlay disabled.

## Transport and rendering technique

Seven explicit cubic Bézier paths are defined in the membrane's undeformed material coordinates. They use unequal origins, radii, lateral bends, widths, phases and warm colour tones to avoid a mechanically uniform radial arrangement.

Each path is rendered as a narrow subdivided ribbon rather than a global surface texture. A small shader supplies a dim molten body and brighter travelling heat pulses. The pulse phase progresses monotonically from the outer origin towards the centre; time never reverses the transport direction.

This approach was chosen because it is the smallest system that makes path identity, direction and surface attachment directly inspectable. It does not require a fluid simulation, particle cover, post-processing or changes to the membrane material coordinates.

## Surface association

The centre and both edges of every ribbon are sampled through the same `membraneHeight(x, z)` function used by the v0.1 geometry. When depression depth, depression width or channel width changes, the ribbon geometry is rebuilt from its original material path and resampled against the changed membrane.

The ribbons sit only `0.035` world units above the calculated surface to avoid depth fighting. Inspection with the diagnostic overlay enabled showed that they continue to follow the same triangulated descent without unrelated UV distortion, floating or clipping.

## Useful ranges

- Path curvature: the final paths use lateral control-point offsets of roughly `0.9` to `3.6` world units. The initial offsets around `0.4` to `1.5` were technically sound but still read as radial spokes from the elevated view.
- Flow speed: approximately `0.5` to `1.1` gives legible inward movement. Lower values become too static; values approaching the maximum `1.8` weaken the sense of weight.
- Lava intensity: approximately `0.9` to `1.6` balances concentrated heat against the dark surface. Higher values remain useful for diagnosis but flatten colour variation and dominate the restrained membrane.
- Channel width: approximately `0.14` to `0.24` keeps paths readable as material while preserving their individuality. Very narrow channels resemble illuminated wires; very wide channels obscure the underlying slope.
- The default combination—speed `0.7`, intensity `1.2`, width `0.18`—is a convincing balanced starting point.
- The v0.1 default surface values remain effective. A stress test at depth `6.5` and width `4.4` also retained correct ribbon attachment and stable normals.

## Perception without diagnostics

The molten paths visibly bend down the inner slopes and outline different cross-sections of the well. A central orange point light and three restrained slope lights provide local response on the dark membrane without turning it into a brightly illuminated terrain. Both the low monumental silhouette and elevated structural view are substantially clearer than the unassisted v0.1 surface.

The diagnostic mesh remains off by default and is not used as part of the artwork.

## Behaviour at the centre

Ribbons narrow and become more concentrated as they approach the centre. During their final five percent they fade away into a small dark circular receiver with a luminous rim. This makes arrival and disappearance unambiguous while avoiding an invented underground environment.

The receiver is a provisional endpoint, not a complete throat. It does not show vertical falling motion or space below the membrane.

## Weaknesses and next implication

- The explicit paths convincingly converge but do not physically merge into shared fluid channels; overlapping arrivals remain separate ribbons.
- The shader represents moving heat concentrations over a continuous molten body rather than simulating changing fluid volume.
- Additive ribbons do not cast general-purpose emissive light. A few deliberately placed point lights approximate their contribution to nearby slopes.
- At high intensity the paths can become visually smooth and electrical rather than viscous. Moderate speed, varied width and restrained intensity give the strongest molten reading.
- The central receiver is visibly symbolic. It proves that surface-bound transport reaches and leaves the membrane, but it cannot show how that transition occurs.

A separate Casting Pit v0.3 throat/transition study now appears justified. Its task should be to replace the provisional receiver with a convincing transition from surface-bound ribbons into depth. No v0.3 implementation was begun in this session.
