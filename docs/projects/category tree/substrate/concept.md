# Substrate

## Concept

The substrate is the dark, energetic medium beneath the finite Casting Pit terrain. It is not ordinary water, another terrain layer or empty black space. It is a textureless pressure sea: a lower world capable of carrying depth, motion and latent energy beyond the constructed membrane.

The Category Tree needs this medium because its gravity well, buried Cosmos and unseen root system imply a world larger than the visible ground. The substrate gives that implication material presence. It remains subordinate to the Tree and molten Universe in the complete composition, but it must be convincing when encountered on its own.

## Visual and kinetic language

The surface combines broad swells, crossing pressure folds and localized moving energy. Large forms establish mass; fine optical activity discloses their motion without replacing it. A nearly black material becomes legible through restrained warm and cold grazing responses rather than a conventional sky, environment map or image texture.

Motion should resemble interacting pressures and currents. It should not read as a texture sliding across a plane, evenly repeated sine rows or a familiar terrestrial ocean. The principal rejected readings are:

- black sand or a shifting desert;
- liquid chrome;
- a calm blue sea;
- regular computer-generated ripples;
- darkness so complete that the surface is only technically present.

The medium may appear lumpy, viscous or unfamiliar. It is not required to behave exactly like water because it represents energetic dark matter rather than a literal seascape.

## Relation to the composition

The Casting Pit terrain owns the visible ground and ordinary occlusion. The substrate owns the colour, movement and energetic depth beneath it. A narrow composition adapter places the shared surface below both the outer terrain datum and the planting envelope's deep-medium boundary. A conservative crest allowance keeps every possible swell beneath those boundaries, while an irregular radial sink conceals the numerical square at the edge of the finite simulation.

Its illumination is material-local. The cold key, cross-current and warm edge that reveal the sea are reconstructed inside the substrate material and cannot relight the Tree, molten layer, information network or terrain. This preserves the stronger standalone study without importing an alien scene-light rig into the completed artwork.

The substrate also supplies one of the four localized influences manifested by the [Superstrate](../superstrate/concept.md). That relationship is a field of influence in the upper composition, not a claim that the substrate engine directly drives the other simulations.

## Motion and controls

The accepted study exposes seven perceptual controls:

- swell height;
- pressure energy;
- crest tension;
- cross-current;
- fine activity;
- pressure reflection;
- current speed.

Together they distinguish form, optical disclosure and time. Freeze is a diagnostic state used to inspect geometry; stillness is not the intended final character.

## Development provenance

The first composition-bound dark-matter experiment was too quiet, too dark and too entangled with the gravity well to judge honestly. Increasing its controls produced something closer to shifting black sand than an energetic sea. The surface was therefore disconnected from the composition and developed as an independent study until its material, movement and local illumination worked in their own right. Only the accepted shared engine was then returned through a composition-specific adapter.

That sequence is part of the artistic method: make the medium convincing before asking it to coexist with the rest of the world.
